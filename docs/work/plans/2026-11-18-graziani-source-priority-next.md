# Tripoli motorization: loading-stage movement boundary

**Date:** November 18, 2026. Priority source question from the [scenario extent queue](2026-11-18-graziani-scenario-extent-crosswalk.md): whether a supply-bearing Motorization allocation can move out of Tripoli in the same OpStage as a loading action, and how that differs from a unit already loaded at scenario setup. The original Land rules contain a specific box restriction not resolved by merely knowing stock or a general movement allowance. No route, action policy or reachable shipment is selected.

## Direct source and actual setup hooks

- Land §8.88, printed/PDF p. 16, treats the Tripoli/Tunisia boxes as unlimited-capacity Supply Dumps and expressly says a **truck or Motorization Point may not be moved in the Operations Stage in which it loads and/or unloads supplies**. This is a movement restriction on the specified carrier in the specified box context, not an unlimited capacity grant for every unit or a generic outside-box rule. The clause does not itself define whether assigning points to an abstract Supply Unit constitutes loading.
- Scenario §60.33, printed p. 27 / PDF p. 3, lists Italian second/third-line trucks **at Tripoli**: 25 light, 140 medium and 40 heavy. Scenario §59.63(B) converts eligible second/third-line medium/heavy trucks to unattached Motorization Points when logistics is abstracted; air-facility trucks are ignored if Air is abstracted. Section 59.66(C) permits each group of 30 other unattached points in one hex to **begin** carrying an additional regular Supply Unit, and expressly treats Tripoli/Tunisia boxes as hexes for that case. These are optional, eligibility-dependent setup choices, not proof that a particular 30-point group or extra unit was chosen.
- Scenario §60.92(B) separately lists **eight regular Supply Units in Tripoli box**, each 5 Ammo and 60 Fuel. These fixed listed units are not automatically identical to, or additional copies of, any particular optional §59.66(C) loaded group. Preserve stock and cargo identity; do not duplicate a counter merely because both rules exist. The separate [Tripoli air case](2026-11-18-graziani-air-transport-case-next.md) concerns box capacity and an abstract-air fuel hypothesis, not this land-carrier movement rule.
- Land §32.32 requires 30 Motorization Points for a real Supply Unit and restricts attachment/detachment to Organization. Section 32.31 says a Supply Unit may move by land or rail in its arrival OpStage; §32.33 limits the carrier's owner-phase movement and provides a non-phasing qualification. Neither directly states that every assignment is a §8.88 load/unload event or that §8.88 is waived for an arriving or initially loaded Supply Unit.

## Conditional distinction

If an accepted action contract establishes that a named Motorization group **loads or unloads supplies in a Tripoli/Tunisia box during OpStage S**, §8.88 says those points may not move in S. A separate hypothetical group that **began the scenario already carrying** a §59.66(C) Supply Unit has no in-stage loading event established by that setup text alone. Whether initial loading should nevertheless count against the first OpStage is an explicit timing question, not a source-proven answer. In either case, enough points, eligible stock, origin identity, phase, location, fuel, off-map movement, control, enemy state and remaining conditions must be checked.

A later arriving Supply Unit presents a separate interaction: §32.31's same-OpStage land/rail permission and §8.88's load/unload movement restriction can both matter if a real loading event in a box is established. Their cited words do not establish whether the permission overrides the box restriction, whether a particular arrival is preloaded, or whether attaching points is loading. Do not impose an automatic same-stage block solely because a unit arrived, or waive a proven loading restriction solely because §32.31 permits some movement. Actual naval arrival and the first-turn startup boundary are separately unresolved.

Land §§8.83–.85 give off-map movement and map entry procedures; a proposed movement still needs its box-to-box or map-entry state and applicable time. An initial preloaded group versus a group attached during Organization, an actual load in S versus no load, and a Supply Unit arriving in S are distinguishing synthetic cases, not certified legal moves. Tests after a decision must preserve carrier and cargo identities and event timing; no path or result follows from a missing event record.
