# Abstract supply destruction: CPA basis

**Finding:** §32.17 charges half the CPA of “all units in the hex,” rounded upwards, but gives no worked example or explicit allocation rule. General unit, attachment and CPA definitions constrain possible interpretations without resolving whether rounding occurs per counter or on a sum, which side's counters are charged, or how a parent and represented subsidiaries are charged. Non-transferable CP makes an aggregate number alone insufficient to determine individual charges.

## Source clues

- [Land rules](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), printed/PDF p. 43 §32.17: players may attempt to destroy Supply Units “they are with”; the player expends half the CPA of all units in the hex, rounds upwards, and rolls for each Supply Unit in that hex. The section does not explicitly say “friendly,” “combat,” “phasing,” “each unit,” or “sum” in the charge clause. Its before-movement, per-sub-segment timing and supply-target scope remain separately unresolved. §32.13 says an enemy combat unit entering a Supply Unit's hex captures it; that constrains some enemy co-occupancy cases, but does not prove all mixed-owner states impossible.
- Land p. 5 §3.1 defines a **unit** as a counter capable of expending CP, separately defines a combat unit, and defines attached as a smaller unit in the same hex represented by its parent's counter. An assigned unit can be elsewhere and need not be attached. An HQ can contain independent subsidiaries represented by the HQ counter or by separate counters; parent units may themselves belong to parents. Land p. 9 §4.25 says an attached unit has no separate counter on the map and, unless otherwise specified, assigned units beginning a scenario in their parent’s hex are attached. This is evidence against counting off-map component counters as separate map occupants, but represented units do not cease to exist for all purposes, and these definitions do not settle how §32.17 debits attached components.
- Land p. 12 §§6.11, .13–.17: each unit has an allowance; certain gun units printed CPA 0 use CPA 10 for purposes other than movement. A parent formation's CPA is the **lowest** of its component units' CPAs, with a movement example; this is a potential parent-basis clue, not an explicit §32.17 parent debit example. CP do not carry into the next Operations Stage and are **not transferable between units**. Motorized infantry may assume its carrying truck's CPA; the example changes 8 to 20. Neither assuming that CPA nor organizational attachment proves a pooled transferable budget or a rule for separately counting carrier and carried unit.
- Land p. 43 §32.0 says players in the abstract game do not receive trucks, but receive Motorization Points to increase non-motorized units' CPA or transport Supply Units. §§32.32–.33 specify the points required to transport real versus dummy Supply Units and where they may move. Truck counter rules from the detailed game therefore cannot simply be assumed to supply additional §32.17 chargeable units in this mode. Motorization Points, the Supply Unit counter, and the transporting combat unit remain distinct concepts; their exact charge treatment is not specified by these passages. The special Supply Unit terminology and general unit definition do not settle whether Supply Units enter the charge basis or what CPA they would have.
- Land p. 43 §32.21 counts individual battalion-equivalents supplied and illustrates a division expending **ammunition** by component counters rather than as a whole. It supplies an example of an expressly stated component-based rule, but does not state a §32.17 CPA calculation. Section 6.14 tracks expenditure across both players' portions of an Operations Stage; §6.21 and errata-corrected §8.17 address separate overrun consequences/limits. Neither removes the need to decide actor and charge allocation for this action.
- [September errata](https://www.spigames.net/db_pages/ERR_CampaignforNorthAfrica.pdf) §32.0 warns abstract rules were untested; no identified §32.17 CPA-basis clarification in the inspected errata. This is a bounded check of available sources, not an exhaustive later-edition search.

## Decisions and discriminating examples

For two independently represented hypothetical eligible units each with CPA 15, half of their combined CPA rounded once is 15; half of each separately rounded up is 8 + 8 = 16. Charging 15 in aggregate does not itself say how 15 CP is allocated without transferring CP, so it is not a complete executable interpretation. The per-unit alternative is coherent with separate unit accounts but is not expressly selected by §32.17. Also distinguish current allowance from unspent CP, parent CPA from constituent CPAs, and printed zero-movement gun CPA from the applicable non-movement allowance. These are synthetic arithmetic cases, not proof either stack or action is legal.

Before admission, explicitly decide subject set and ownership/authority, attached versus detached representation, motorization, parent and child accounting, per-unit versus aggregate rounding and allocation, overrun restrictions, and target/dummy treatment. Preserve unsupported cases as unknown; do not infer legality or leak hidden units from a partial snapshot. Proposed tests should contrast separate versus attached children, odd CPAs, an eligible non-movement zero-CPA gun, motorization, differing owners, insufficient ordinary remaining CP and replay/privacy. These are proposed policy tests, not executed results.
