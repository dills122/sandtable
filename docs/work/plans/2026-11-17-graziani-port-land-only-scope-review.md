# Graziani Land-only port scope: independent review

**Date:** 2026-11-17
**Disposition:** Accept threshold and unresolved mode-scope distinction; strengthen
§32.11 evidence and qualify the proposed mutual-exclusion test. No port policy
or implementation is established by this review.

## Findings

1. **§32.11 is stronger scope evidence than resource abstraction alone.** Its
   last sentence says only the supply rules given below apply. The §32.0 general
   rule also expressly applies when playing the Land Game without the Air and
   Logistics Games. These support limiting detailed supply rules in Land-only
   mode. Yet §32.36 explicitly retains a destination efficiency condition and
   does not supply a complete efficiency lifecycle. Neither passage alone
   resolves whether §30.57 applies to abstract supply-unit shipments, other
   retained Commonwealth transfers, or neither. Record that direct scope text
   alongside the plan's narrower resource-expenditure discussion; do not infer
   an automatic import or repeal of every detailed port rule.
2. **Keep threshold and cargo interaction separate.** §32.36 asks whether the
   destination's current efficiency permits at least 50% of listed capacity;
   exactly half qualifies. It does not itself impose a tonnage ledger or an
   incoming-SP deduction. Errata §30.57 instead corrects the Commonwealth
   transfer rule: each incoming SP reduces stage maximum tonnage by 10%,
   outgoing SP do not; its 1,700-to-1,530 Tobruk example does not establish
   actual opening throughput. Its reciprocal provision has a 50% supply
   threshold and an *at least* one-third SP reduction for ports with incoming
   capacity over one SP; one-SP maximum is excepted. Erratum literally says
   “Air incoming capacity,” so its wording and intended scope warrant explicit
   interpretation. Chart §55.3 states handling tonnage and SP does not affect
   the other; §55.15 describes a possible supply-efficiency effect from troop
   debarkation. Their overlap, precedence, and Land-only application remain open.
3. **Keep substitution endpoints explicit.** §32.36 permits one Commonwealth
   supply unit per OpStage by sea to/from Alexandria, Benghazi and Tobruk,
   during Naval Convoy Arrival, in lieu of combat-unit transport “from the port
   of origin and into the port of destination.” The plan's proposed “one-unit
   mutual exclusion” vector should preserve those endpoint words and test any
   broader interpretation only after its scope is settled. The provision does
   not alone prove control, availability, or reachability.
4. **The 2/5 example remains conditional.** Scenario §60.7 says Tobruk starts
   at efficiency 7 with San Giorgio partially sunk; chart §55.3 lists 5 and
   indicates starting efficiency below five, while §55.25 gives a three-level
   reduction. These conflict. §55.14 provides proportional scaling in detailed
   rules; if chart 5, blockage 3, and that scaling are selected, 2/5 fails and
   3/5 passes. No source here authorizes silently subtracting three again from
   the scenario's stated starting 7. Source precedence, any above-maximum rule,
   recovery, blockage clearance, cost, timing and rounding remain decisions.
