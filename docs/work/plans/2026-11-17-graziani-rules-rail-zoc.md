# Commonwealth rail and enemy ZOC: source interaction

**Disposition:** Land §10.26 supplies substantial textual support for a friendly-combat-unit exception to rail ZOC effects, but §8.73 states an unqualified rail-specific prohibition. Do not admit either precedence or a self-occupancy rule without a versioned decision. This is a bounded source analysis, not an official correction, executable rule, or proof of a reachable six-turn rail trip.

## Printed facts

- [Land rules](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), PDF/printed p. 15 §8.73: a rail traveler starts the OpStage on a rail hex, must not have spent CP for another reason during that OpStage, and must not be in an enemy ZOC. It travels along the line and cannot enter an enemy ZOC **or** enemy-occupied hex at any time while using rail movement.
- Same scan, p. 18 §10.26: for **all movement purposes**, a friendly combat unit's presence in a hex negates the *effect* of an enemy ZOC. Its wording does not say that the enemy ZOC itself ceases to exist or that enemy occupation is negated. Section 10.27 separately recognizes opposing units exerting ZOCs on each other and mutually controlled hexes; §10.23 explicitly references §10.26 as an exception to its retreat prohibition. Section 10.29 explicitly permits truck convoy entry into an enemy ZOC if already occupied by a friendly combat unit; this is evidence of a convoy-specific exception, not itself a rail rule.
- Section 10.21, same page, limits ZOC across all-sea, major river, lake and escarpment sides and into an adjacent hex the projecting unit could not enter. A neighbor alone does not prove enemy ZOC. For rail movement, §8.76–.78 on p. 16 also restrict destroyed/unbuilt track, pickup/dropoff, and Commonwealth use of rail hexes west of an Axis-combat-unit-occupied rail hex. An exception to one ZOC effect would not alone remove these separate restrictions.
- Section 24.65, p. 37, separately bars building **or** rebuilding an enemy-controlled or enemy-occupied railroad hex. Section 10.26 addresses movement purposes; a selected movement exception does not by itself authorize construction in a controlled hex. Sections 24.61–.63 and .67 add builder eligibility, construction or repair procedures, and a no-skipping westward extension requirement.
- Section 10.16, p. 18, requires the non-phasing player to disclose whether a potentially relevant unit exerts ZOC when a phasing unit starts a **Movement Segment** in, or moves into, that hex. Section 5.2 names rail movement as a separate phase; applicability of this disclosure to rail requires its own decision, not automatic disclosure of hidden state on every rejected command.
- [September errata](https://www.spigames.net/db_pages/ERR_CampaignforNorthAfrica.pdf), PDF p. 1 §8.73 adds that units traveling by rail may not earn reorganization points in that stage. Its §8.71 correction permits Axis use under §54.4; it does not state a rail ZOC exception. No explicit §10.26 correction found during inspection of its four pages. Other material may exist.

## Competing readings and choices

1. **Broad movement exception:** apply §10.26 to the enemy-ZOC prohibitions in §8.73 because rail is movement and §10.26 says all movement purposes. A friendly combat unit already present in a route hex would negate that ZOC's *movement effect*, while §8.73's distinct enemy-occupied prohibition remains. This reading has direct textual support; it does not itself specify the representation of presence at an instantaneous rail crossing.
2. **Rail-specific prohibition:** apply §8.73 strictly to any enemy ZOC, even one containing a friendly combat unit, on the theory that a specific rail case limits a general rule. The source does not expressly state that priority; it conflicts with a literal inclusive reading of “all movement purposes.” Record that tension instead of presenting either as printed errata.

Starting-hex and entry checks are distinct. Under reading 1, decide whether the traveling combat unit's own presence can satisfy §10.26 at its starting location, or whether another friendly combat unit must already occupy it. The clause says presence of *a* friendly combat unit, without expressly requiring a different unit; allowing self may make the starting ZOC bar ineffective for any qualifying combat traveler, whereas excluding self adds a qualification. On entry, assuming the traveler itself can first enter and thereby negate a prohibition risks circularity; an **already present** independent combat unit is a bounded possible policy, supported by §10.29's convoy language but not expressly specified for rail. Supply counters and other non-combat travelers must not be treated as friendly combat units without a separate classification. The time at which occupancy is checked, pickup/dropoff, unit classification and sharing a rail stack remain admission questions.

Do not use this analysis to waive enemy occupation, Axis west-of-rail restriction, damaged/unbuilt track, stage-wide CP, or friendly stacking restrictions. Section 8.73's stage-wide CP language also needs an explicit decision on later expenditure, not just earlier spending. Physical enemy ZOC, movement effect, and control should remain separate concepts; a friendly combat occupant need not make a controlled hex noncontrolled for other purposes. Unknown incoming ZOC, qualifying-occupant identity, boundary and track coverage must not be treated as clear. Disclosure of a rejected route can reveal hidden enemy state; establish side-safe command and event behavior separately.

## Proposed tests after decision

Use certified synthetic rail paths, holding all other restrictions constant: enemy ZOC at origin, intermediate hex and destination, each with no friendly occupant, a separate friendly combat occupant, or a non-combat occupant; compare traveling combat and non-combat units at origin. Exercise §10.21's blocked projection separately. Verify enemy-occupied hex remains barred even with a friendly combat occupant; verify §8.78 independently of ZOC and retain destroyed-track/CP restrictions, including selected later-CP semantics. Test §24.65 construction separately in enemy-controlled and enemy-occupied cases. Test unknown projection and occupant status as unknown, not absent, plus deterministic replay and observations under the separately chosen §10.16 disclosure policy. These vectors distinguish policy choices and are not assertions of historical reachability or passing tests.
