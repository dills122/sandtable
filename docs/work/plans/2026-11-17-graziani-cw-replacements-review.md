# Independent review: Commonwealth replacement planning and motorization

**Result:** The six-turn offset table and ordinary Replacement Point arrival ban
are supported. Two qualifications need adding before either analysis becomes a
policy: §20.73 supplies another printed arrival-date instruction for random
production, and §§20.74 and 32.51 materially strengthen the case that the
pre-November ban affects production-based motorization. Neither point proves
that scheduled whole-unit reinforcements disappear or that any actual plan,
roll, or motorization arrival occurred.

## Findings

1. **Planning-period lookup has one more explicit conflict.** Land §20.73,
   printed/PDF p. 32, says random infantry and truck points received per turn
   depend on the *date of arrival*. Errata §20.72, PDF p. 2, changes production
   table lookup to the month/turn in which arrivals are planned. The infantry
   chart §20.78B expressly keys Date to the planning turn; §20.78C likewise
   keys availability to planning turn. These support the proposed corrected
   planning-time interpretation, but the review should name §20.73 as
   additional conflicting printed wording, not imply §20.72 was the only
   arrival-date language. The truck chart §20.78A headings say game-turns
   1–30 and 31–107 and its note says four turns hence, but its note does not
   independently define the heading date as explicitly as §20.78B/C do.
2. **Motorization scope analysis omits stronger source language.** Land
   §32.51 p. 44 says Motorization Points are treated in all aspects as Medium
   Truck Points except as modified in that major case. §32.52 explicitly calls
   this rate-based source Motorization Points *as Replacements*. Further,
   §20.74 p. 32 discusses splitting replacement points and uses 10 Medium
   Trucks as an example. These cut against treating medium-truck production as
   plainly outside §60.47 simply because §20.0/.2 do not list truck class.
   The proposed ordinary replacement ban is well supported; the alternative
   rate-equivalence reading remains an unresolved interpretation, not an
   equally source-established entitlement. It must also explain whether
   prohibited truck-point arrivals leave any actual medium-truck ability on
   which §60.92(A)'s rate can operate. Preserve distinct scenario-provided
   reinforcement motorization under §32.52; the ban alone does not dispose of
   it. No authoritative resolution of this interaction was found in the
   inspected errata.
3. **Arrival mechanics need their own source checks.** Chart §20.78A calls
   for three rolls, one per truck type, and limits Alexandria to 25% of total
   trucks produced in a turn; it says production does not affect trucks
   arriving as reinforcements. These provisions are relevant if a policy
   actually models the physical truck production procedure. They do not, by
   themselves, prove that the Alexandria limit or each physical truck roll
   carries over unchanged to abstract motorization. Avoid automatically
   applying or discarding them until the abstraction and location are resolved.

## Confirmed arithmetic and conditions

The source calendar places GT1–6 at September III–IV and October I–IV. On the
stated arithmetic convention, +2 targets GT3–8 and +4 targets GT5–10,
respectively; the original table computes all six rows correctly. Scenario
§60.22 starts GT1 OpStage 1 and ends GT6 OpStage 3, while §5.2 places normal
planning before operations: a GT1 planning row is a hypothetical planning
slot, not proof that a preamble was executed or a plan exists. Scenario
§60.47 bars Replacement Points arriving before November 1940, so none in the
ordinary listed categories arrives during GT1–6. Planning for later turns is
not itself an arrival, and post-scenario plans are not proven obligations.

§5.2 says two turns; §20.21 and both random production chart notes say four;
corrected §20.72 says one month, with a first-July-phase example. Four turns
and one calendar month match numerically in the presented row sequence, but
that does not alone settle which phases generate plans or all partial-month
cases. Chart §20.78B starts infantry planning at GT3; chart §20.78C dates and
its monthly limits match the reviewed examples. §32.81 removes Commonwealth AA
replacement points, so a printed AA chart row is not a Land-only arrival.

Original §4.43a track lists 7 Indian Brigade at GT5 OpStage 1 with 5 light and
20 medium trucks, and 6 New Zealand Brigade at GT6 OpStage 3 with 25 light,
85 medium and 5 heavy trucks. These are correctly separated from chart
production; exact Land-only crosswalk is not established by those rows alone.

Useful discriminators: (a) a recorded GT2 medium production roll of 1 gives
10 medium trucks in chart column GT1–30, nominally GT6 under +4, but not a
proven abstract arrival; (b) a GT3 infantry roll of 3 selects 3 infantry
points on §20.78B, nominally GT7 under +4, outside the scenario; (c) GT3
planning against GT5 under +2 remains barred by §60.47. All are hypothetical
rolls, conditional on the relevant phase and adopted interpretation. None
substitutes zero for missing records. For cases (a) and (b), verify location,
stage, and any other applicable restrictions independently.
