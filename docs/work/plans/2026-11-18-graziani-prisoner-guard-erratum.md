# Prisoner guards: September erratum and Graziani scope

**Result:** September errata §28.17 changes the moving-prisoner ratio to **one Guard Point per five Prisoner Points**. The same case's separate one-Guard-Point-per-detention-camp requirement remains printed. A Graziani setup contains infantry formations from which guards might be formed; the checked starting entries identify no Prisoner Point, chosen Guard Point or detention camp. This bounded inspection does not establish a surrender, a guard, a movement, or their six-turn reachability.

## Original and correction

[Land rules](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), printed/PDF p. 40 right column, §28.17 says each detention camp requires one Guard Point; its next sentence originally requires one Guard Point per **1** Prisoner Point when moving. [September errata](https://www.spigames.net/db_pages/ERR_CampaignforNorthAfrica.pdf), PDF p. 2 middle column, §28.17 changes the **1 in line four to 5**. Read against the printed sentence, this corrects the moving ratio, not the camp's first sentence. The text does not specify rounding for fewer than five or other nonmultiples; choose no rounding policy from this correction alone.

Land §28.11 creates one Prisoner Point for each surrendering infantry TOE Strength Point, not for tanks or guns. §28.12 permits immediate movement up to three hexes under its restrictions; §28.13 gives prisoners an eight-CP movement allowance in the captor's Convoy Movement Segment. §28.14 allows a detention-camp marker if more than five points are standing in a hex. §28.22 forms Guard Points from an **infantry-type unit** by removing a TOE Strength Point and substituting a guard counter; each represents one infantry TOE point. Guards may react or retreat before combat but prisoners do not. Section 28.23 provides escape for abandoned or excess prisoners; the consequences of a specific partial guard allocation depend on the unresolved ratio rounding and actual state. Sections 28.16 and .24 describe departure and escapee procedures separately. None establishes a mandatory camp at five or fewer, a free new infantry point, or an actual capture.

## Graziani and abstract supplies

[Scenario](https://spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_Scenarios_DupFromAirRules.pdf), printed p. 27 / PDF p. 3, §§60.22, 60.31 and 60.41, runs GT1 OpStage 1 through GT6 OpStage 3 and lists Italian infantry divisions and Commonwealth infantry entries. These are **candidate sources** for guard formation, subject to their actual infantry TOE, ownership, location and applicable rules, not proof a Guard Point was created. In particular, a unit name containing “Guards” is not itself evidence of a §28.22 guard counter. The visually checked initial land and supply listings on scenario pp. 27–29 do not specify prisoners or a camp. That bounded reading cannot rule out all scenario text, initial implicit state, subsequent surrender, or the legal six-turn route from combat to escort. Establish these facts before testing an actual guard or escape.

Land §28.15 requires one Store Point per five Prisoner Points in a hex per Operations Stage, with its own source language. Land §32.0/.11, p. 43, applies to Land without Air and Logistics and directs players to ignore ordinary usage and expenditure of Ammo, Fuel, Stores and Water, replacing supply accounting with §32. Thus no ordinary §28.15 Store debit should be inferred in that mode solely from detailed-game text. Whether any separate prisoner availability, feeding or escape consequence survives abstraction needs an explicit cross-case interpretation: §28.24 separately discusses water and escapee survival, while §32.11 addresses usage/expenditure. Do not invent an abstract prisoner supply charge or infer an automatic escape or death.

For later validation, distinguish one established camp guard from movement escort; compare exactly five moving prisoners with ten under the corrected ratio, then separately examine nonmultiples only after a rounding decision. Establish an actual infantry surrender and source TOE, guard formation and debit, timing, movement path and legal status first. These are synthetic comparisons, not executed tests or scenario witnesses.
