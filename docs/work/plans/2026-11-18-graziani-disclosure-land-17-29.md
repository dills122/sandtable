# FG-01: disclosure census, Land §§17–29

**Result:** A bounded visual sweep finds explicit replacement-roll secrecy and several desert-raider revelation triggers beyond the previously inventoried addendum and minefield cases. They are not a complete digital visibility contract. In particular, a dummy dump's status is revealed under §27.56 only **after any raider–guard conflict, if a raider survives**; do not generalize that trigger to every entry, capture or mode.

Read the [limited-intelligence inventory](2026-11-18-graziani-limited-intelligence-source-inventory.md) and [minefield comparison](2026-11-18-graziani-minefield-disclosure-source.md) first. Land addendum §3.61 restricts opponent counter information unless expressly indicated; its §3.62 examples are partial. The following is source text and bounded interaction, not an adopted policy or proof any unit/action exists in Graziani.

| Original Land case | Trigger and source-described observation | Limits / interaction |
|---|---|---|
| §17.55, p. 28 | The owner may attempt material destruction **only after declaring** voluntary surrender of units in a hex. | The clause establishes declaration order; it does not itemize recipients, exact disclosed inventory or digital event payload. Actual surrender and destruction legality have independent conditions. |
| §§18.12–.13, .21, p. 28 | Phasing player designates reserve units by placing a Reserve I marker atop them; those retained at first Reserve Release either leave reserve or flip to Reserve II. The marker indicates current reserve status. | This is physical marker placement/face, not explicit permission to disclose hidden component contents or complete future intent. |
| §20.0 general rule, p. 31 | **Any Replacement die rolls may be kept secret from other players, including members of the same team.** | Optional secrecy for the roll, not an automatic prohibition on every later observation or a rule that replacement counters, totals and outcomes are always invisible. Land-only motorization/replacement scope is separate. |
| §§21.42, 21.52–.55, pp. 34; addendum §3.62, p. 45 | Breakdown places a numbered marker and records information on owner's sheet; capture and potential instant towing have specified predicates. See [captor review](2026-11-18-graziani-vehicle-marker-captor-source-review.md); no blanket claim of invisible marker or captor ignorance follows. |
| §§24.31–.34, .38, 26.11, .14–.15, .23, pp. 36, 38 | Construction secrecy and real/dummy front/reverse, enemy-entry type reveal and competing dummy-removal timings are checked in the [minefield review](2026-11-18-graziani-minefield-disclosure-source-review.md). | No fresh policy is inferred from project costs, marker placement or conflicting timing; retained here for census continuity. |
| §§24.56, 24.66–.67, p. 37 | An optional **End of Road** marker indicates road construction extent; destroyed railroad hexes have a Destroyed RR Hex marker; a Railhead marker indicates new railroad extent. | Marker instructions concern board representation. Section 3.62's secret **item undergoing construction/demolition** remains relevant; the text here does not specify an opponent-facing project history or all timing. Do not read optional road marker as mandatory. |
| §§27.31–.33, pp. 38–39 | At initial formation a desert-raider counter is placed on the map; afterward the owner secretly records its movement and location, normally off-map. The rule does not define all player-memory, digital position or historical trail audiences. Formation and availability are independent gates. |

A named target, mode and valid raid must be established. §§27.53–.54 concern detailed Air facilities/aircraft; they do not by themselves create those actions in Land-only play. §27.55/.56 dump versus abstract Supply Unit interaction needs a mode decision. |
| §27.58, pp. 39–40 | On the specified convoy-raid die result, the defender chooses the lost truck type while the raider chooses the cargo type lost. | The [review](2026-11-18-graziani-disclosure-land-17-29-review.md) identifies this asymmetric choice; it does not itself authorize a complete cargo manifest or establish Land-only applicability. |
| §27.88, p. 40 | When SAS stacks with an LRDG, SAS is removed from physical sight and moves like an LRDG until escorted to placement. | This is conditional on SAS/LRDG existence and interaction; it does not itself define every opponent-visible event or Land-only availability. |
| §28.14, p. 40; addendum §3.62, p. 45 | Prisoner Point markers remain on the map until departure; if more than five points are standing in one hex, captor **may** place a POW detention-camp marker. Do not infer a different marker policy from one threshold without source support. |
| §29.1, p. 41 | Initiative holder rolls two dice for stage weather and a further die for affected sections when applicable. | Printed procedure does not itself specify all digital audience/timing semantics; actual result is state. Detailed aircraft effects are separately mode dependent. |

§§19, 22–23 and 25 yielded no additional explicit audience/reveal clause in this bounded visual pass; this is not proof of absence of every indirect information effect. Sections 17, 18, 20, 21, 24, 26–29 have the material cases above; source-specified board changes are distinguished from express secrecy/revelation. The current §3.6 review and prior marker/minefield reviews remain relevant rather than being marked complete by this census. Unreviewed detailed Air/Logistics rules, charts, scenario overrides, replay views, logs, error channels and other exceptions require their own checks.
