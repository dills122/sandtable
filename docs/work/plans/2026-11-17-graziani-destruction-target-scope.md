# Abstract supply destruction: target and action scope

**Finding:** Land §32.17 says a player may attempt to destroy Supply Units they are with and calls for a die for **each Supply Unit in the hex**. That wording favors an all-in-hex procedure; it does not expressly authorize selecting a subset, define ownership or dummy treatment, or reconcile the all-unit instruction with a counter already attempted in the same sub-segment. Do not turn any one interpretation into an admitted legal action without resolving those boundaries.

## Source evidence

- [Land rules](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), PDF/printed p. 43 §32.0, govern abstract supply when Land is played without Air and Logistics. §32.12 represents Supply Units by Supply Dump counters. §32.14 gives them zero stacking value; §32.15 defines real ammunition/fuel capacities and removal when both reach zero. A counter's face alone does not prove its underlying inventory or whether it is real.
- §32.17 says players may attempt to destroy Supply Units “they are with,” charges one-half CPA of **all units in the hex** rounding upward, and calls for one die for **each Supply Unit in the hex**. Adjusted 1–3 destroys the unit; other results leave it unaffected. Add one if the sum of stacking points in the hex during the attempt is one or less. Attempt occurs before any movement in a given sub-segment and is limited to one per Supply Unit per sub-segment. These are hex-wide words alongside a unit-specific limit; the paragraph does not state that a player can omit an otherwise covered Supply Unit, choose a second subset later, or roll again for an already attempted unit. The source does not define the sub-segment's mapping to the sequence, or resolve how to proceed if one unit is already ineligible. CPA allocation and rounding are also separate unresolved matters.
- §32.13 says a Supply Unit has no combat capabilities and is captured if an enemy combat unit enters its hex, with supplies used immediately and freely. This entry rule does not itself define the ownership and durable state of all subsequent counters, the legality of occupied-hex entry, or which counters an actor may select for destruction. §8.13 p. 14 generally bars entry to an enemy-occupied hex; their interaction needs a specific ruling. Do not assume an enemy-owned co-located unit is always possible or impossible, automatically assign ownership on a hypothetical state, or conflate capture and destruction. See [capture analysis](2026-11-17-graziani-supply-capture-next.md).
- §32.18 authorizes dummy Supply Units at the stated ratio, moves them like real units with less motorization, and removes them on discovery. It does not explicitly say whether a dummy in an attempted hex receives a die, whether an attempt itself discovers it, or whether its removal happens before an all-in-hex determination. §32.32 distinguishes 30 motorization points per real unit from six per dummy for transport. Those facts do not authorize disclosing identity through an option, cost, or public event.
- [Common chart](https://spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_ChartsBothPlayers.pdf) p. 1 §6.3 lists one-third CPA to blow a supply dump; Land §32.17 specifies one-half, while §32.12 uses Dump counters. [Cost review](2026-11-17-graziani-destruction-cost-hierarchy-review.md) explains why scope and precedence remain a policy question; neither formula nor the detailed §54.14 dump procedure settles abstract target selection. Do not silently combine costs.
- [September errata](https://www.spigames.net/db_pages/ERR_CampaignforNorthAfrica.pdf) PDF p. 2 §32.0 cautions that abstract rules were not tested. As documented in the [independent audit](2026-11-17-graziani-supply-destruction-review2.md), no direct §32.17 correction was identified in the bounded pp. 1–2 inspection. Other editions, later clarifications and indirect provisions remain possible.

## Admission questions and checks

Before any action is offered, choose a source-justified rule for actor and ownership, meaning of “with,” eligibility of every counter in the hex, real/dummy discovery and ordering, already-attempted targets, subset versus compulsory all-in-hex handling, and cost/stack snapshots. An all-in-hex proposal must still address a counter prohibited from another attempt; a subset proposal must acknowledge that §32.17 says each. Neither becomes source fact merely because it simplifies an interface. If hidden authoritative data or the chosen scope is unavailable, fail closed without exposing why.

The [independent review](2026-11-17-graziani-destruction-target-scope-review.md) notes that withholding a side-visible offer based on hidden real/dummy or stack facts can itself disclose those facts. A generic rejection is insufficient. For authoritative histories indistinguishable to that side under the accepted knowledge and discovery rules, require observation-equivalence across offers, rejection categories, event counts and IDs, timing, and subsequent observable RNG effects. Specify an authorized disclosure or a safe protocol first; if neither is established, keep the capability unavailable without state-dependent visible gating. This is a software privacy requirement, not printed source language.

Test two real units with independently determined dice, mixed real/dummy, an already-attempted unit alongside a fresh one, contested or captured ownership, and zero/one/multiple valid acting units. Check once-per-window identity, paid CP, stock/carrier outcomes, replay, and capture/discovery ordering. Compare side-authorized projections across equivalent observable histories, including action offers, errors, stable IDs, event counts, RNG effects and timing. These are conditional discrimination vectors, not claims of legal placement or executed tests.
