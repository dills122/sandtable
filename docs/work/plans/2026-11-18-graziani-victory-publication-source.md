# Graziani terminal timing and disclosure: source boundary

**Date:** November 18, 2026. Follow-up to the [victory route proposal](2026-11-17-graziani-victory-policy-next.md) and its [review](2026-11-17-graziani-victory-policy-review.md). Original text establishes a scenario endpoint and restricted opposing information, but these passages do **not** specify a digital terminal publication event, audience, or handling of an unresolved decision. No publication policy is selected here.

## Direct source observations

- Scenario §60.22, printed p. 27 / PDF p. 3, says Graziani's Offensive ends with the **conclusion of Game Turn 6, Operations Stage 3**. It directs victory to §60.71, while the printed Graziani victory list is §60.81 on printed p. 28 / PDF p. 4. The linked work treats this reference correction as a project interpretation; it is not a source-supplied correction. Section 60.81 lists victory conditions, but does not state a separate scoring moment, terminal cleanup order, publication audience or interface.
- Land §5.2, printed/PDF p. 12, places player A's patrol phase after movement, convoy, applicable rail and repair; then player B performs F–L. Both players repeat the stage facets in the third Operations Stage. Its ensuing “End of Game-Turn” passage says the turn is complete and supplies no named victory, reporting or cleanup procedure in that outline. This supports treating the end as after both players' stage portions, but does not establish the ordering or applicability of every independently specified end-of-stage obligation. No additional cleanup event should be inferred from the outline alone.
- Scenario introduction §59.0, printed p. 25 / PDF p. 1, discusses broadly worded victory conditions and expresses the designer's expectation that a player knows whether objectives were accomplished. It is commentary, not an instruction to disclose opponent state, witnesses, timing, or a computed outcome to a particular audience. The paper game's shared rules are accessible to players, but that does not settle what source-private information they receive at terminal evaluation.
- Land addendum §§3.61–.62, printed/PDF p. 45, restricts knowledge of opposing-counter status, composition and attributes to express rule disclosures and then lists examples, including a patrol-specific restriction on supplies and attached trucks. It does not provide a victory-specific exception or specify the audience of a verdict. That limited list and the inspected scenario victory section do not establish a universal postgame secrecy rule or prove that no other source authorizes disclosure.

## Consequence and open questions

The printed endpoint is stronger than an arbitrary “end of turn 6” label: the third Operations Stage, including both players' portions, must reach its conclusion under the adopted sequence. The exact snapshot relative to any applicable additional stage-end work, and whether a game can terminate or an objective can be assessed earlier, are not answered solely by these passages. Section 60.81's take-and-hold, supply and victory-level interpretation remains separately unresolved; this note does not decide it.

Neither source directs publication immediately upon proving all rows, nor prescribes a pending response, later disclosure, audience, evidence access, or whether availability/timing is itself visible. The timing leak identified by the [policy review](2026-11-17-graziani-victory-policy-review.md) therefore remains an explicit design/admission question, not a source-approved secrecy or announcement scheme. Paired cases where hidden state changes decision completeness can test an eventual chosen audience contract, but cannot establish what the original rules authorize.
