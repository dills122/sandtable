# Initial contact edges: national-border rule check

**Finding:** The checked Land movement and ZOC passages do not assign a separate cost or prohibition to a political border. The blue checkered line on Fort Maddalena's `C3019–C3020` side is a strong Libya–Egypt boundary lead, not a direct visual match to the key's `Border` example. This does not prove unrestricted movement or ZOC on that side, nor eliminate independent terrain, unit-entry or scenario constraints.

## Printed map and contact edges

Visually rechecked [Map C and terrain key](https://spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_Maps.pdf), PDF pp. 3 and 1. The checkered blue mark follows the `C3019–C3020` common side at Fort Maddalena. The terrain key's `Border` example instead shows a plain gray hexside with neighboring grid rays; it does not depict blue checks and may mean a generic hex border rather than the marked national side. The geographically supported political interpretation, with country-label continuity and reproduction instructions, is documented in [boundary source](2026-11-17-graziani-border-mark-next.md) and [independent review](2026-11-17-graziani-border-mark-review.md). Treat that as interpretation, not a key-certified symbol identity.

For the other fixed opposing pairs, the separately inspected local source notes find the blue line west of the `C3419–C3520` diagonal and `C3419–C3320` diagonal. The former has a track crossing; the latter has partly obscured grid under the place name. See [1st RTR side](2026-11-17-graziani-map-first-rtr-side.md) and [Sheferzen boundary](2026-11-17-graziani-map-sheferzen-border.md). Do not infer the hidden stretch free of coincident terrain or extend these observations to other hexes.

An [independent same-scan review](2026-11-17-graziani-contact-border-rules-review.md) confirms the key/ink distinction and cautions that a bounded absence of a named border rule does not establish passability.

## Bounded rules check

- [Land rules](https://spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), printed/PDF p. 14 §8.19, specifically prohibit Commonwealth land units moving west of Marble Arch `A2109` for any reason. This geographic and nationality restriction is not worded as a Libya–Egypt border ban and does not itself identify any of the three Map C sides as prohibited. No claim that every reachable route obeys it follows.
- Printed pp. 14–15 §§8.31–.37 and .41–.43 address terrain, hexside and movement effects; the inspected §8.37 chart and its errata do not specify an independent national-border cost. Printed p. 18 §10.21 forbids ZOC across all-sea, major-river, lake and escarpment sides, and separately into an adjacent hex the projecting unit cannot enter. A political-border name is not among those enumerated side exclusions. This bounded negative check does not prove no restriction elsewhere, no coincidence with other terrain, or a legally enterable destination.
- [Scenario](https://spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_Scenarios_DupFromAirRules.pdf), PDF p. 3 / printed p. 27 §60.31, fixes Sheferzen Garrison in `C3419` and separately uses `Anywhere in Libya` for other units. A setup-region predicate is distinct from an ongoing prohibition on crossing the line, and explicit placement is distinct from an inferred country classification. It cannot be generalized to all units or scenarios. See [fixed contact inventory](2026-11-17-graziani-fixed-contact-inventory.md) for printed pair positions and unresolved optional placements.

No checked passage establishes an additional political-border effect on these sides. Preserve the mark and its uncertainty, assess any coincident side feature and each unit's entry and ZOC independently, and review additional nationality/scenario rules before a positive legal-action conclusion. No automatic terrain type, passability, ZOC or control follows from this note.
