# Patrol stage history: source review

**Disposition:** Supports the [stage-history follow-up](2026-11-18-graziani-patrol-stage-history.md)'s bounded reading and its separation of source evidence from selected RUL-GRA-19 planning policy. The printed sequence and worked example support distinguishing declared attacks from resolved steps; they do not themselves settle the patrol gate for every probe, defender participation, or later combat. Same-scan informed review, not an independent edition or a runtime ruling.

- **Probe wording:** §15.25 expressly calls a below-threshold attack a probe and says it is not a Close Assault, with a reference to §16.0. §15.91 describes a probe as a Close Assault with less than the relevant 50% of available strength. §5.2's Close Assault step resolves assaults and reveals which were probes afterward. These passages support competing readings for the patrol restriction; the explicit §16.0 cross-reference is meaningful evidence for the distinction, not a complete reconciliation of the contradictory wording. A separate anti-armor event could independently matter.
- **Timing and actor:** §5.2 places A's patrol phase after A's repeated movement/combat sequence and before B repeats phases F–L. It instructs both players to resolve any anti-armor fire simultaneously; therefore defending participation in A's portion is possible, and B's later actions are not yet observed at A's patrol. §16.0 uses past-occurrence wording, while §16.21 describes whether the phasing player engages in the stage; Phase L uses phasing-player assault wording. This supports the proposed distinctions but does not unambiguously define engagement, actor scope, or whether future B combat bars A's earlier patrol. A past-events-only treatment is a stated planning choice, not the only source reading.
- **Retreat example and automatic outcomes:** §6.14's 1st RNF example has Italians move and attack, then the Allied unit retreat before assault; neither side spends CP to defend/close-assault in that sequence. It supports distinguishing an announced attack from execution and does not itself adjudicate a patrol. §15.29 explicitly says no assault when the attacker allocates no units under its stated condition; defender zero-allocation instead causes automatic retreat and disorganization. The latter does not expressly decide the patrol-history classification. No CP expenditure or table roll alone is a universal combat-occurrence rule.
- **Barrage:** §16.21 permits patrol despite artillery barrage but bars reconnaissance of a hex that has undergone barrage or air bombardment. Its target-hex sentence does not expressly repeat a stage window or assign an actor; current-stage scope remains an interpretation, not a quotation from that sentence.
