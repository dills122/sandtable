# VI-07: intrinsic HQ weapons and victory combat-unit status

**Result:** A named Italian regimental HQ with a listed gun supplies a concrete source case for §3.34, distinct from Aresca's tank-equipped HQ. The checked rules still do not expressly say whether an HQ with its own gun but **no attached combat units** qualifies as a combat unit for §60.81. Do not derive the victory predicate merely from a weapon rating, its ability to fire, or the glossary's situational exception.

## Source-specific candidate

Scenario §60.31, [scenario book](https://spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_Scenarios_DupFromAirRules.pdf) PDF p. 3 / printed p. 27, places **1st Libyan Division at C4020**. Its [Axis OA](https://spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_ChartsAxisPlayer.pdf) PDF p. 5 lists **1st Libyan Regiment HQ**, ID `h`, `1 × 65/17 Gun`, deployed `D`, and lists infantry battalions under that formation. Axis §4.46b PDF p. 26 describes `h` as an HQ which may assign one artillery TOE point; §4.48 PDF p. 29 gives the 65/17 a barrage rating of 5 and close-assault 1/1. The OA supplies the weapon's model and quantity, not merely capacity. These are setup-source baselines; actual representation, attachment, gun survival, ammunition, position and valid reorganization must be established for the state being judged. The division row does not assert that this HQ starts at or can reach a victory objective by the terminal snapshot.

## Classification and conditional alternatives

- [Land rules](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), pp. 5–6, §3.1 defines combat units by capability and notes situational exceptions for HQs, engineers and dummies. §3.23 lists combat unit types while allowing certain noncombat units to count for named determinations such as ZOC and reconnaissance; it does not expressly list §60.81 victory among those examples.
- §3.31 explicitly considers an HQ counter a combat unit **if any combat units are attached**. §3.33 says an HQ without attached combat units is not a combat unit, occupies zero stacking points and cannot voluntarily attack; its parenthesized ratings have their own conditions. §3.34 separately says certain HQs, mostly Italian regimental HQs, with directly assigned artillery may use their combat strengths and revert to noncombat HQ status when all gun TOE are eliminated. This is evidence for a special armed-HQ reading, but it does not explicitly state how its reversion language overrides §3.33's no-attached-combat-unit classification for a victory predicate. Presence of a listed 65/17 alone is not a published victory-specific exception.
- For a **hypothetical** 1st Libyan Regiment HQ with an eligible attached combat battalion, §3.31 supports classifying the HQ counter as a combat unit representing attached units, subject to actual representation and other applicable rules. For the same HQ with no attached combat units but an intact assigned gun, compare (a) §3.33's literal no-attachment result and (b) a scoped §3.34 armed-regimental-HQ exception based on its use/reversion wording. The checked sources do not explicitly choose between those readings for victory; no project choice is made. Gun eliminated, replaced or HQ no longer independently represented requires a separately evaluated state, not extrapolation from the hypothetical.
- §3.36's automatic-capture rule concerns an HQ with **no combat values, whether parenthesized or not**, no combat units in its hex and an enemy combat unit placing it in ZOC. September 1979 errata p. 1 clarifies that HQs with parenthesized combat values may defend. This is a capture/defense rule, not an express rule that every defending HQ satisfies a victory predicate.

Scenario §60.81's Italian **strategic** condition explicitly names a combat unit holding Alexandria or Cairo that can be supplied by convoy from Map D; its **decisive** condition likewise names a combat unit for Mersa Matruh and Siwa, with the Tobruk supply condition. Other categories have their own printed wording and must not inherit a combat-unit quantifier solely from these examples. Even an accepted HQ classification cannot prove occupation, the correct objective, qualifying route, target snapshot or final outcome. §32.16's friendly-combat-unit supply-draw subject is another separate applicability decision, not a substitute for §60.81's convoy language.

For Aresca, the [CV33 review](2026-11-17-graziani-blocker-aresca-cv33-review.md) confirms one HQ CV33 and §3.35's armored-HQ restriction; [participation review](2026-11-17-graziani-aresca-hq-participation-alternatives-review.md) retains its nominal/shell classification and combat-use interpretation. The gun-equipped Libyan example tests **§3.34**, while Aresca's own tank tests **§3.35**; neither resolves the other. A named official victory-specific clarification or example of an unaccompanied armed HQ would discriminate the interpretations. No such clarification was identified in the checked passages; this is not an exhaustive errata or later-edition search.
