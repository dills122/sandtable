# Conditional retreat overlap: salt-marsh source witness

**Disposition:** A synthetic state can satisfy the textual premises of both Land §§15.34 and 10.36, subject to the state qualifications below. This identifies competing consequences, not their timing, precedence, or a historical Graziani position. It addresses the projection objection in the [independent continuation review](2026-11-17-graziani-compulsory-combat-continuation-review.md).

## Source basis

- [Land rules](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), printed/PDF p. 15 §8.44: motorized units and AFVs cannot assault units defending in salt marsh. Most vehicles may enter or leave marsh only on a road or track; specified light trucks, recce-type units and motorcycle infantry are exceptions. An otherwise prohibited vehicle entering without a track is abandoned. Section 8.33 requires a connecting road/track hexside to use that movement benefit; §8.32 also states the vehicle restriction.
- Printed/PDF p. 18 §10.21(a–c): all-sea, major-river, lake and escarpment sides block projection; projection also cannot enter a hex the projecting unit could not enter from its present position. Its example explicitly denies a tank ZOC into marsh without a connecting road or track. This restriction tests **enemy projector's entry into the phasing unit's hex**, not the motorized attacker's ability to enter the defender's marsh.
- Printed/PDF p. 23 §15.34 prohibits motorized full assault or probe against marsh defenders, and requires a combat unit in the ZOC of an enemy it cannot attack to retreat one hex unless another unit attacks that enemy. Printed/PDF p. 18 §§10.31–.36 specify attacks against the relevant enemy-occupied hexes, allow qualifying Holding Off, and prescribe three-hex retreat if a unit can neither assault nor Hold Off. Section 10.31 does not say every individual phasing unit must attack. The [independent review](2026-11-17-graziani-retreat-overlap-source-review.md) records that distinction. The [September 1979 errata](https://www.spigames.net/db_pages/ERR_CampaignforNorthAfrica.pdf), p. 1, limits §10.3 to the phasing player.

## Synthetic state and checks

Use axial hex coordinates; adjacent coordinates differ by one of `(1,0)`, `(0,1)`, `(-1,1)`, `(-1,0)`, `(0,-1)`, `(1,-1)`.

| Hex | Assumed state |
| --- | --- |
| `E = (1,0)` | Salt marsh, occupied by a non-phasing, non-motorized infantry combat force. It meets all §10.11–.15 ZOC size, raw defensive strength and cohesion requirements; it can legally enter `P` from `E`. |
| `P = (0,0)` | Clear terrain, occupied by a phasing motorized combat unit with an offensive assault rating, but no ability to barrage or otherwise attack `E`; it remains motorized at the decision point. It is not solely an excepted gun/noncombat force or pinned under §10.32. |
| `(-1,0)`, `(-2,0)`, `(-2,1)` | Clear, empty and traversable by `P`; all relevant sides unblocked. No other enemy units or ZOCs. |

The `E`–`P` side is ordinary, with no all-sea, major-river, lake or escarpment feature. Non-motorized `E` can enter clear `P`; assume no other entry restriction, so the independently qualified `E` force projects into `P` under §10.21. Neither reciprocal projection from `P` nor a road into `E` is necessary for that conclusion. `P` has no other friendly unit available to attack `E`, no qualifying Holding Off barrage, and no applicable exception; §8.44 and §15.34 bar its assault or probe against `E`. No alternative attack is assumed. Thus §15.34's unable-to-attack and §10.36's neither-assault-nor-Hold-Off predicates both hold on these stated facts, before deciding their interaction. A motorized infantry unit unloaded from trucks would require a new eligibility assessment; do not assume it stays motorized or loses the restriction automatically.

A geometry check for a possible three-step route is `P → (-1,0) → (-2,0) → (-2,1)`. All three entered hexes are outside `E`'s adjacent ZOC, and the endpoint is exactly three hexes from `E`; distances from `E` along the route are 2, 3, 3. Under assumed clear terrain and no other enemy forces, this avoids enemy ZOC and vehicle marsh-entry problems. Charge applicable movement CPs and any movement-generated disorganization; §10.36 adds three disorganization points. If forced into enemy ZOC, §10.36 instead specifies surrender; §10.26's friendly-occupant exception must be checked in any different fixture. The first step also supplies a possible one-hex retreat geometry, without deciding whether either consequence applies first or whether other rules determine a route.

**Negative control:** replace marsh `E` with a higher hex across an escarpment from motorized `P`; assume no other enemy ZOC. Even though §15.34 bars its uphill motorized assault, §10.21(b) blocks `E`'s ZOC through that side in both directions. Adjacency alone therefore establishes neither overlap nor §15.34's ZOC premise. A second enemy projecting into `P` needs separate evaluation of which enemy cannot be attacked and whether another unit attacks that enemy; it cannot simply be substituted for `E` in this witness.

The cited passages do not expressly settle ordering or precedence between the one- and three-hex prescriptions, nor identify a route-selection algorithm. Preserve those questions as unresolved rather than applying both retreats serially or selecting a winner. This is a conditional rules fixture, not proof of a reachable actual map position, unit roster, scenario state, or exhaustive errata review. No runtime/data changes or gameplay tests.
