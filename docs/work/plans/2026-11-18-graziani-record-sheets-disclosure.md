# FG-01: log sheets and limited intelligence

**Result:** Land §4.5 describes record sheets as aids for extensive information, including a need to keep vital information secret from other players. Its detailed sheet descriptions allocate forms by player **usage**, while §4.51 says that grouping does not determine who actually uses them. Neither that rationale nor the contents of a sheet establish a blanket rule that every field is secret or that an opponent may inspect the whole sheet. The separate §3.61–.62 addendum and specific case exceptions remain relevant.

## Printed records and audience boundaries

| Source | Record and described user | Disclosure boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Land §4.5–.51](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), pp. 10–11 | Counters carry little combat information; sheets store extensive data. Keeping vital information secret from other players is one stated reason. Four groups are organized by their “owning” player's usage, explicitly a presentation decision with **no effect on who actually uses what**. | This is a rationale and usage classification, not a complete field-by-field opposing-player or teammate access rule. The reference to “other Players” does not itself settle all team roles or specific exceptions. |
| §4.52, pp. 10–11: Field Commander control and TOE sheets | Frontline-combat players use these forms. Control-sheet fields include CP expenditure/allowance, cohesion, combat points, ammo and fuel; TOE sheets record parent and subordinate strengths, ratings, attachments, trucks and supplies. Each parent has its own TOE sheet. | The sheet may contain information that §3.61–.62 restricts, but its field list does not alone specify each opponent entitlement. Section 4.52 also says the player may record information in a chosen manner so long as **he and the other Players have no problems reading it**. Its context concerns legibility; it does not expressly resolve which players may inspect which hidden entries. Reconciling this general wording with the specific secrecy/reveal cases is an interpretation, not permission to expose a complete sheet. |
| §4.53, p. 11: Rear Area Commander forms | The player handling construction, demolition, mopping up and other rear-area work uses this group. Vehicle Repair sheet handles broken-down, destroyed and abandoned vehicle counters; Naval Convoy sheet records transport, location, destination, transit and cargo; POW sheet handles prisoners, camps and guards. | Addendum §3.62 expressly makes broken/destroyed/abandoned vehicle **marker contents** secret, and POW/camp/guard numbers plus Axis nationality secret. It does not prove all sheet fields or markers have identical audience or duration. [Captor review](2026-11-18-graziani-vehicle-marker-captor-source-review.md) leaves capture disclosure open. |
| §4.54, p. 11: Air Marshal forms | Player in charge of airplanes uses squadron and air mission sheets; fields include airfield/squadron location, composition, aircraft state and missions. | The §3.62 aircraft examples specify particular combat and bombing/strafing entitlements, not universal access to these sheets. Detailed aircraft and SGSU activity is not created by Land-only abstraction. |
| §4.55, p. 11: Logistics Commander forms | Player in overall charge of supplies uses truck/major-item, supply dump, supply net and requisition forms. Truck sheet records number/type, cargo, empties and transported items; dump sheet tracks supplies and location; net sheet tracks present, incoming and anticipated supplies. | Section 3.62 expressly keeps truck and Replacement Point numbers, types and other details secret. This does not automatically settle all supply values, planned arrivals, cargo after capture or authorized disclosures. Land §32.2 allows the Supply Dump, Truck Convoy and TOE sheets to track abstract ammo/fuel; retain the separate Land-only mode scope and case-specific procedures. |

Land §3.61 p. 45 bars knowledge of opposing counters' status, composition and other attributes except as expressly indicated; §3.62 calls its list partial. Its map passage allows a player to examine a stack of counters while distinguishing what is inside them. The subsequent listed anti-armor, close-assault, patrol and other cases supply particular exceptions and limits; visible counters or a sheet's legibility do not automatically convey its private contents. Conversely, a source-specified disclosure must not be suppressed merely because its source value also appears on a log sheet.
