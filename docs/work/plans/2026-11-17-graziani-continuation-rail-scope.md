# Graziani continuation: nonlocal rail witness scope

**Disposition:** Treat certified unbuilt printed rail as no §8.78 witness. Prefer excluding the historical Soluch–Benghazi–Barce line as a versioned interpretation, supported by its explicit lack of terrain or movement effect. Destroyed constructed rail remains an unresolved witness policy: §8.76 bars rail entry but does not erase the hex. Qualify the Axis occupant under the actual combat-unit and representation rules, not by ZOC or an arbitrary strength threshold. No trip or historical reachability is established.

## Source and interpretation

- [Land rules](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), printed/PDF pp. 15–16, §8.78 prevents Allied railroad movement use of a rail hex west of **any** rail hex occupied by an Axis combat unit. No same-line, route membership, proximity, travel direction or ZOC requirement appears in that sentence. Off-route does not mean off-rail: a qualifying eastern rail occupant can be relevant even if the intervening route is clear. Which geographic relation means west, what counts as using a hex, and the applicable state snapshot remain separate decisions.
- Section 8.47, p. 15, says unconstructed railroad hexes are ignored for all purposes; §24.67, p. 37, says unbuilt railroad hexes do not exist or function until built; §8.76 bars use before construction. An Axis combat unit on a certified unbuilt printed railroad hex therefore does not supply a §8.78 rail witness. Unknown construction status is unknown, not an unbuilt finding. Completing construction may change the result once its own timing and other prerequisites are established.
- Sections 8.71 and 24.6, pp. 15 and 37, identify the Soluch–Benghazi–Barce line as historical only; §8.71 says it may not be used for anything and has no effect on terrain or movement, and §24.6 says the Benghazi–Barce line may never be used for any reason. Excluding this line's ink as an independent §8.78 witness is a strong reading of those broad restrictions, but is still an application of them to an indirect nonlocal movement effect; §8.78 does not expressly name this exception. Record it as an adopted versioned interpretation, not an explicit erratum. Where another qualifying line actually occupies the same hex, assess that independently; historical ink does not erase other established rail. Separate line identity and overlap from map transcription uncertainty.
- Section 8.76 calls destroyed locations rail hexes and prohibits their entry by rail movement until repair. Section 24.66 describes destruction and markers, not disappearance of railroad. Neither passage expressly resolves whether an Axis combat occupant there is a §8.78 witness for a different, otherwise usable hex. Two plausible policies remain: retain it as a rail hex despite inoperability; or require an operational witness. The former follows the noun and keeps §8.76's entry restriction distinct, but neither is presented as a definitive source ruling. Repair and destruction timing need separate treatment. Do not extend §8.47's unbuilt rule to destroyed rail automatically.
- Section 3.23, printed/PDF p. 6, defines combat units by infantry, tank, reconnaissance, artillery, anti-tank and anti-aircraft types, while allowing certain non-combat units to be treated as combat units for particular determinations. It lists examples but does not expressly name §8.78. Section 3.31 says an HQ counter represents attached combat units and is itself considered a combat unit when any are attached; §3.33 distinguishes an HQ without attached combat units. Hence an unattached non-combat counter alone is not automatically a witness; a represented attached combat unit cannot be discarded merely because its map counter is an HQ. Determination-specific exceptions and their application to §8.78 remain separate questions. Do not substitute whether the stack projects a ZOC, can attack now, or has positive combat strength for that ruling. Section 8.73 independently forbids rail entry of an enemy-occupied hex and enemy ZOC; it is not the same predicate. See the [independent review](2026-11-17-graziani-continuation-rail-scope-review.md).
- [September 1979 errata](https://www.spigames.net/db_pages/ERR_CampaignforNorthAfrica.pdf), p. 1, §8.71 permits Axis use under §54.4 despite the original Commonwealth-only statement. It does not expressly revoke the historical line's separate exclusion or change the Allied restriction in §8.78. The §8.73 addition concerns reorganization-point earning, not witness scope. No §8.78 clarification identified in this errata; this is not an exhaustive census of later publications.

## Conditional test vectors

All vectors use independently established western usable rail, east/west relation, snapshot and otherwise legal fixtures. They are specifications for future tests, not executed tests or evidence of Graziani reachability.

1. Eastern off-route constructed rail with an Axis combat occupant blocks western use under §8.78; replacing it with a classified non-combat occupant alone does not satisfy that predicate. Exercise both travel directions and the separate §8.73 path occupancy/ZOC checks.
2. Same witness hex certified unconstructed: no §8.78 witness. Constructed control blocks; unknown construction status remains unknown absent another independently sufficient witness. After valid construction completes, rerun against the selected timing policy.
3. Historical line only, east of the used hex: no witness under the proposed interpretation. Include overlapping independently qualifying rail as a distinct case, and unknown line identity as unknown. Do not conclude historical ink alone creates a connection or usable path.
4. Destroyed previously constructed eastern witness: assert the chosen explicit policy, with undamaged and repaired controls; keep destroyed-path entry prohibited separately. Do not treat proposed outcomes as source-settled. Test transition timing only after selecting the governing snapshot.
5. Compare independent Axis combat counter, non-combat counter, and HQ with represented attached combat unit; include parenthesized-strength and determination-specific classifications only after their rulings. Combat unit lacking ZOC remains a distinct case from an actual non-combat unit. A second qualifying witness should dominate an unknown candidate internally, subject to any separate observation and causal-closure requirements.
