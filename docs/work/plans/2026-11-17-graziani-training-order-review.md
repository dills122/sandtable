# Training and arrival-stage order: independent review

**Disposition:** credible bounded planning candidate, not an admitted rule or
complete implementation contract. The proposed distinction between whole-unit
morale training and replacement-point arrival-stage training is supported by
the printed order and explicit replacement provision. It remains an
interpretation of their interaction, not an official clarification. Tighten
the absorption boundary and distinguish pending credit from completed training.
This review uses the same edition, not a separate edition or blind transcription.

## Findings

1. **Sequence and replacement exception are accurately distinguished.**
   Land §5.2 puts the Organization phase, including training completion and
   initiation/continuation, before Naval Convoy Arrival in each OpStage. Its
   Organization *segments* may be performed in any desired order, not moved
   across the later arrival phase. §20.12 places whole-unit reinforcements at
   the beginning of arrival, permits same-stage movement and charges no CP for
   disembarkation or the initial placed hex. September errata §8.86 corrects
   the contrary Tripoli/Tunisia-specific movement prohibition, not a general
   Commonwealth Cairo restriction. Those facts support the proposal's
   ordinary-sequence choice that a unit arriving after Organization does not
   start or receive §17.3 morale-training credit in that OpStage. Section
   20.43 expressly permits **replacement points** to count their arrival stage,
   so a blanket prohibition for both categories would erase printed text.
   Whether the cross-reference from replacement training to reinforcement
   training implies an analogous whole-unit exception remains an alternative;
   neither sequence text nor the exception explicitly resolves that extension.

2. **Separate assignment on arrival from absorption at Reorganization.**
   Land §20.41 requires replacement points to be designated for eventual
   delivery to specific units immediately on arrival; §20.49 says points may
   be **absorbed only during the Reorganization Segment**. Under the
   candidate's no-reopening order, that segment already preceded Naval Convoy
   Arrival, so a newly arrived point cannot be absorbed in the same OpStage by
   the ordinary sequence, even if one-stage training is later credited.
   Another express exception would need separate evidence or a versioned
   interpretation. The proposal rightly does not automatically enable the gun
   point, but treats absorption timing as wholly undecided: specify this
   source-backed constraint and distinguish initial destination assignment,
   training completion, movement, and actual absorption. The exact training
   completion step and whether a trained-but-unabsorbed point can take a
   particular action still require their own rules.

3. **CP and movement tests are sound within their subjects.** Section 17.33
   says any CP expenditure during an OpStage interrupts that unit's training,
   without excluding reaction or involuntary expenditure. Section 5.2
   separately bars a unit beginning or continuing training from voluntary
   movement during the remainder of the stage, with reaction excepted.
   Therefore a zero-CP voluntary move is still barred under that phase rule;
   a legal CP-consuming reaction interrupts under §17.33; a zero-CP reaction
   avoids that *particular* interruption criterion but does not guarantee all
   other training conditions. Do not treat reaction as universally legal or
   prove a progress reset from the word “interrupted.” The proposal's
   no-progress-erasure gate is appropriate while interruption consequences are
   unresolved. Source §20.12's no-CP initial placement is specifically stated
   for reinforcements; do not silently generalize it to every replacement
   representation.

4. **Arrival-stage credit is conditional, not a stage-long fiction.**
   Section 20.43 permits the arrival stage to count; it does not specify a
   point's instructor-presence interval or an end-of-stage event. Section
   17.35 requires a qualified combat battalion in the hex during each training
   OpStage and specifies type, morale, strength and stacking conditions. The
   proposal's instructor presence from actual arrival through stage end and
   pending-credit bookkeeping are reasonable **project choices** for the
   missing interval, not printed timing. It also correctly leaves open how
   §17.35's unit-specific instructor requirements apply to each replacement
   class and side. The Land §20.43 schedule is one stage for guns, three for
   infantry and six for tank/AC/recce; its gun note characterizes the process
   as uncrating. Confirm exactly which restrictions apply to each rather than
   assuming a universal instructor or a one-point unit identity. September
   errata §17.3 states Axis training centers train replacements rather than
   actual units; it does not waive those questions.

5. **Vectors need a precise credit/completion distinction.** A pending
   arrival-stage attempt is not itself a credited stage; after the selected
   interval and all accepted prerequisites, one stage may be credited. Make
   missing instructor, ineligible location, spent CP and unknown eligibility
   distinct outcomes: a known failure prevents credit, while unknown blocks
   the dependent authoritative decision instead of certifying failure. Test
   legal and illegal zero-CP reaction, instructor departure/return, missing
   instructor at arrival and stage end, and checkpoint replay around the
   credit boundary. Under the proposed order, add a negative same-stage
   absorption test alongside later Reorganization, while keeping same-stage
   independent movement or defense gated by their own provisions. A synthetic
   last-stage arrival establishes no roster, morale value or actual six-turn
   reachability.
