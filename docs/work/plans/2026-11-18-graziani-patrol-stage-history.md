# Patrol stage history: event classification follow-up

**Date:** November 18, 2026. RUL-GRA-19 already selects a stage-wide **completed past events** and current-stage target-bombardment interpretation for planning, not runtime admission. Its [proposal](2026-11-17-graziani-patrol-policy-proposal.md), [final review](2026-11-17-graziani-patrol-policy-final-review.md) and [whole-stage audit](2026-11-17-graziani-rul19-21-audit.md) retain actor-sensitive, unbounded-target and whole-stage alternatives. This note does not select a new ruling. It examines what counts as a qualifying history event, including a probe and a threatened assault followed by retreat; it does not establish that either occurs in an actual Graziani stage.

## Printed evidence and boundaries

- Land §16.0 allows patrol in an Operations Stage in which no anti-armor or close assault combat **has taken place**. §16.21 instead says the phasing player must not engage in either against enemy units in that stage. Phase L in §5.2 uses phasing-player assault wording and does not separately enumerate anti-armor. These formulations do not decide whether defending counts as the player's engagement or whether a later action within the stage must be precluded. §16.21 allows patrol despite artillery barrage, but its separate bar on a target hex that has undergone any barrage or air bombardment does not explicitly repeat a time window or specify an actor.
- §5.2 orders A's repeated movement/combat segments, then A's convoy, rail if applicable, repair and patrol, then B's corresponding F–L. Each combat segment includes barrage, retreat before assault, force assignment, simultaneous anti-armor and close-assault steps; the phasing player reveals a probe after its resolution. A defender can therefore participate in combat during A's portion, and B's later combat is not yet known at A's patrol boundary. A completed A combat before B's patrol and B combat after A's patrol are distinct temporal cases.
- **Probe classification remains unsettled.** §15.25 says an attack below the specified 50% threshold is a Probe and “not a Close Assault,” explicitly cross-referencing §16.0; §15.91 instead describes a Probe as a Close Assault conducted with less than 50% of available points. §5.2 labels probe within its close-assault step. The wording supports competing treatments of a resolved probe for the patrol gate. Neither classification automatically settles a separately occurring anti-armor event in the same combat segment. Preserve attack type, assigned strengths, actual resolved steps and later probe disclosure; do not infer that an announced probe is the only relevant fact or silently treat all probes as safe.
- **Threat is distinct from resolved combat.** The §6.14 example has Italians move and attack 1st RNF, then the Allied unit retreats before assault; it states neither side expends CP on close assault because retreat occurs first. This is evidence that an announced/threatened attack and an executed close assault are distinguishable in the sequence, not an explicit patrol ruling or proof of an actual scenario event. §15.29 also explicitly says no assault occurs when the attacker allocates no units in its stated circumstances; the defender's zero-allocation case instead requires an automatic retreat. Whether that second result counts as assault for the patrol restriction remains a classification choice, not a table-roll inference. Preserve declarations, assignment, retreat and execution separately.
- An actual resolved anti-armor step is distinct from assault/probe classification. §5.2 says both players simultaneously resolve any anti-armor fire; a planned step with no eligible fire does not by itself establish that fire occurred. Whether zero-effect fire or a no-roll automatic result qualifies as combat is not expressly resolved by these passages. Track actor, type, target, execution and applicable result; do not reduce a combat segment or planned attack to an indiscriminate Boolean.

## Conditional checks, not reachability claims

| Synthetic history | Source consequence or unresolved choice |
| --- | --- |
| A declares attack, defender retreats before assault; no anti-armor or other qualifying combat | §6.14 illustrates absence of a close-assault expenditure in that specific sequence. A patrol still requires separately accepted history interpretation and all eligibility, target, weather and path conditions. |
| A resolves a probe, no anti-armor | §§15.25 and 15.91 leave whether it triggers the §16.0/.21 close-assault gate unsettled. Compare both readings; test after probe disclosure. |
| A conducts anti-armor with B participating; B later seeks patrol | §16.0's stage-wide past-occurrence reading differs from an initiator-only §16.21 reading; a participant reading depends on the meaning of engagement. No side-specific legal outcome is asserted. |
| A patrols before B's later combat | Selected RUL-GRA-19 past-event planning reading permits later combat without retroactive effect if other gates pass; literal whole-stage alternative remains live and requires a separately justified forward, delayed or other mechanism. |
| Current target underwent barrage versus another hex; same target in prior stage | §16.21 distinguishes target restriction from global barrage allowance; current-stage reset and actor scope are interpretations. Compare both sides and prior stages without erasing durable history. |

Check empty assignment, retreat before assault, probe/full distinction, anti-armor with and without fire, zero-effect resolution, attacker/defender participation, canceled declarations, equal boundary order, stage change and later correction. All other patrol conditions, map/occupancy, hidden information and history completeness remain independent gates. These are test proposals, not executed tests or scenario reachability proof.
