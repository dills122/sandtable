# Sequential dice: ordered reading and convoy outcomes

**Disposition:** The source supports reading two distinguished dice in fixed order, not sorting their rolled face values from high to low. The Land glossary's “larger die” can initially be misread as the numerically larger result; Land §15.73 explicitly disproves that reading for its sequential throw. The earlier convoy `55–64` examples correctly include `56`. This is a dice-reading check, not a resolution of 5% cargo grouping or rounding.

## Source evidence

- [Land rules](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), PDF/printed p. 5 §3.1, defines sequential dice with the larger die as the tens figure and smaller die as the ones figure. On that sentence in isolation, “larger” could be misunderstood as a comparison of face values; it can also refer to distinguishable physical dice.
- Same scan, p. 24 §15.73(a), expressly says a **2 on the large die and a 5 on the small die gives 25**. This makes die identity, not numeric value, the intended meaning in that sequential Land procedure. Its close-assault example also uses `34`. The same throw is then added for separate results under §15.73(b–c). An unambiguous ordered d66 integer such as 25 preserves both faces and can be reversed; recording only their sum loses die order. Section 15.73(d) conditionally requires one additional die after a captured result; that close-assault procedure is not a convoy rule.
- Same scan, p. 44 §32.64 says two dice are read sequentially for the Axis convoy result. It does not repeat §15.73's example or separately specify a different sorting method. Applying the common §3.1 definition with the Land example's physical-die meaning supports fixed ordered d66 in this convoy procedure; this remains a source interpretation, not a measured RNG implementation.
- [Air rules](https://spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_AirGameRules.pdf), PDF/printed p. 15 §45.0 procedure, explicitly illustrates sequential `2`, `4` as `24`. Its p. 16 air-combat example treats `25` and `12` as possible. These are independent rule contexts in the same publication, not an explicit amendment of Land convoy rules; they corroborate distinguished-order reading and rule out numeric descending sorting for those examples.
- [Common chart](https://spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_ChartsBothPlayers.pdf), PDF/printed p. 12 §41.5, Axis Naval Convoys: 21–40 bomb points and `55–64` yield 5%; valid distinguished d6 outcomes in that interval are `55`, `56`, `61`, `62`, `63`, `64`. At 201–260 bomb points, `64–66` yield 25%: `64`, `65`, `66`. These are chart lookup examples, not proof of reachable bomb totals or actual convoy cargo. The September errata §41.5 fixes the separate Barrage Points heading; the inspected text does not identify a §3.1 or §15.73 dice correction. Other errata remain possible.

Assuming two independent fair six-sided dice with fixed distinguishable roles, the conditional lookup probabilities for those particular cells are `6/36` and `3/36`, respectively. Fairness, independence and the chosen bomb column are assumptions; none follows solely from the result ranges. Sorting numeric faces would omit 56 and other tens-less-than-ones results and change many outcomes; do not implement that sorting on the strength of the glossary alone.

## Implications and limits

Preserve die roles or a reversible ordered d66 value in the recorded roll; an unordered sum alone is insufficient. Distinguish the two roll orders `(5,6)` → 56 and `(6,5)` → 65, test replay and ruleset version, and verify the admitted RNG/dice contract separately. For an attack actually at 21–40 bomb points, 56 reaches the 5% cell; a valid lookup does not settle §32.65's missing explicit 5% rounding, cargo composition, allocation, secrecy or physical reachability.
