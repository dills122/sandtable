# Graziani continuation: rail restriction beyond the route

**Disposition:** Land §8.78 supplies an independent, nonlocal Commonwealth rail restriction. A route-only enemy-occupancy or ZOC check cannot establish that restriction is satisfied. This narrows the evidence needed for the existing rail decision matrix; it does not select ZOC precedence, settle westward geometry, admit a rail action, or prove reachability.

## Source and consequence

- [Original Land rules](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), printed p. 16, §8.73 prohibits a rail traveler from entering an enemy-occupied hex or enemy ZOC, and requires a rail-hex start and no other CP expenditure during the Operations Stage. Section 8.77 permits pickup and dropoff along the line subject to its stacking, direction and CP restrictions. Section 8.78 separately says the Allied player may not use a rail hex **west of any rail hex occupied by an Axis combat unit**. The [September 1979 errata](https://www.spigames.net/db_pages/ERR_CampaignforNorthAfrica.pdf), p. 1, adds no reorganization points during a rail-travel Stage to §8.73 and clarifies Axis rail use under §54.4; it does not expressly amend §8.78.
- Consequence: take a synthetic west–east sequence of certified rail hexes `A–B–C–D`, with the entire proposed Commonwealth trip `A–B`, no enemy unit or ZOC on that trip, and an Axis combat unit occupying `D`. If `A` and `B` are west of `D` under the accepted geographic relation and `D` qualifies as a rail hex under §8.78, §8.78 bars use of `A` and `B`; the enemy need not occupy the traveled portion. This is independent of the unresolved §8.73/§10.26 ZOC hierarchy. The source says **use**, so treat pickup, dropoff and traversed rail hexes as candidate uses; origin-only treatment or an exemption for the other direction needs an explicit ruling, not an inference from a clear route.
- Conversely, an Axis non-combat unit alone does not satisfy §8.78's named combat-unit condition. Enemy occupation on the entered path still triggers the separate §8.73 rule, regardless of that classification. Likewise §10.26's movement-effect negation does not remove an Axis occupant or itself waive §8.78. Multiple Axis units require only one qualifying eastern witness to bar a western used hex; no assumed chain of control or ZOC is needed.

## Remaining decisions and evidence

The printed text does not define a coordinate comparison for “west,” especially equal-longitude cases, bends, map seams or off-map boxes. An [independent review](2026-11-17-graziani-continuation-rules-review.md) identifies §8.47's instruction to ignore unconstructed railroad hexes for all purposes, strongly supporting their exclusion here absent an overriding rule. Treatment of destroyed railroad and the instant governing multiple pickup/dropoff steps remain separate questions. Do not silently substitute rail graph order, increasing map column, distance to Alexandria, ownership or ZOC for geographic west. Verify map coordinate semantics and track status with source evidence; if unresolved, retain alternatives as versioned policy choices. This analysis does not interpret Axis rail under §54.4.

For a selected policy and fixed authoritative snapshot, model the §8.78 predicate as an existential witness: a qualifying Axis combat occupant on a qualifying rail hex east of at least one hex used by the trip establishes a block. An unblocked result needs complete enough rail, occupancy, classification and west-relation coverage to exclude every such witness; a clear path alone cannot prove that. Unknown potentially qualifying off-route rail or occupancy remains unknown unless another known witness already proves blocked. A later boarding or dropoff must use the selected event ordering and snapshot; no conclusion follows about whether separate moves or a protector train are otherwise legal.

§10.16 disclosure addresses a Movement Segment; the separate rail phase and hidden off-route occupants require an explicit audience and timing policy. A hidden eastern witness must not leak through options, response categories, timing, downstream play or replay. If no authorized observation-independent protocol exists, keep the public action boundary unavailable until it does. This is separate from certification of terrain, track construction, stacking, ordinary enemy occupation, CP/reorganization and the ZOC choices already listed in the [decision matrix](2026-11-17-graziani-decisions-batch-matrix.md).

## Discriminating synthetic tests

- Put one Axis combat unit east of an otherwise clear `A–B` path: §8.78 blocks even though the unit is off-route. Reverse relative positions, test equal longitude and a rail bend under the selected west definition, and vary movement direction while holding used hexes fixed.
- Compare a combat occupant with a non-combat occupant at the same off-route hex; then place each on an entered path to exercise §8.73 separately. Exercise multiple eastern occupants and remove one at a time.
- Test missing eastern-rail or occupancy coverage as unknown, a known eastern witness plus unrelated unknown as blocked, and a certified exhaustive absence as passing this gate only. Compare destroyed/unbuilt-rail status under each explicit policy; do not interpret a missing record as absence.
- Cover pickup, transit and dropoff, independently verified legal stage sequencing, snapshot changes and replay. Use paired hidden histories with identical authorized observations but different off-route Axis occupancy; compare choices, responses, timing, state and later behavior. None of these fixtures proves historical reachability.
