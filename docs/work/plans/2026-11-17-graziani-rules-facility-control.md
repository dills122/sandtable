# Tobruk repair facility: control and destruction interaction

**Disposition:** initial temporary-versus-major conflict remains unresolved after control changes. Land §24.81 adds a material distinction: enemy-unit presence can destroy a *temporary* facility, while a *major* one cannot be destroyed that way. This is not an explicit rule converting one type to another, nor proof of capture or a legal repair attempt.

## Source facts

- [Scenario](https://spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_Scenarios_DupFromAirRules.pdf), PDF p. 3 / printed p. 27, §60.33 places an Italian major repair facility at Tripoli and temporary facilities at Tobruk and Benghazi. Section 60.31 on that page places Italian forces at Tobruk at setup.
- [Land rules](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), PDF/printed p. 35, §22.31 identifies two facility types, says scenario-designated facilities include temporary facilities at start, and lists major facilities in Tripoli, Tobruk **for whoever controls it**, Alexandria and all Cairo hexes. Its location-wide Tobruk statement conflicts with the specific scenario's initial temporary designation. Section 22.32 requires a vehicle to begin the Maintenance Segment at a facility and not have been towed during that OpStage; additional repair eligibility remains.
- Land p. 37 §24.81 says only temporary repair facilities may be newly constructed, while major facilities already exist; bombing and barrage can reduce or neutralize either. Temporary facilities may be destroyed by enemy-unit presence, but major facilities may not be destroyed **in that manner**. The sentence does not specify a mandatory automatic destruction event, whether presence is evaluated relative to original owner or current controller, a timing boundary, or whether a separate surviving major facility exists at a scenario-temporary site. Other rules may bear on these questions.
- Land p. 37 §24.85 permits a temporary facility in any major-city hex or village and caps each player at two temporary facilities. This allows examining coexistence in principle but does not establish a second initial facility at Tobruk. Section 24.87 bars new temporary construction in enemy-controlled hexes, yet permits facilities to be rebuilt or dismantled there. Sections 24.83–.84 give distinct construction and rebuilding procedures; abstraction and their interaction with §24.81 require decisions. Neither rebuilding permission nor construction placement establishes eligibility to repair vehicles under §22.13.
- Land p. 35 §22.34 uses separate temporary and major broken-down-vehicle repair columns. Sections 22.36–.37 treat weather and ZOC differently by facility type, with an explicit temporary-facility exception for major cities in §22.37. Land p. 34 §22.13(a) has a separate enemy-control restriction and major-facility ZOC exception; their full interaction remains a separate policy question. Do not equate this with the distinct Tobruk port-efficiency conflict.
- [September errata](https://www.spigames.net/db_pages/ERR_CampaignforNorthAfrica.pdf), PDF p. 2, corrects cross-references in §§22.34 and 22.8. No explicit §22.31, §24.81 or §60.33 correction was found in inspection of its four pages. This does not exclude another official clarification or an indirectly relevant case.

## Interpretations and lifecycle gates

[RUL-GRA-07](2026-11-12-graziani-ruling-decisions.md#rul-gra-07-bounded-facility-and-victory-planning-policies) selects one temporary facility at initial Tobruk for planning. If admitted, at least these post-control readings remain distinguishable:

1. **Continuing scenario facility:** initial temporary type persists until a separately defined destruction or construction event. Enemy presence triggers only the separately adjudicated §24.81 mechanism; no major facility is silently created by control transfer. This preserves the scenario override but limits the literal reach of §22.31.
2. **General location after setup:** scenario designation controls only the starting snapshot; §22.31 supplies a major facility for the subsequent controller. The printed texts do not say precisely when it appears, whether it replaces a surviving temporary facility, or how prior damage is carried forward. A simple conversion or creation rule is a project choice.
3. **Coexisting facilities:** a general major facility exists alongside the scenario temporary facility. The two source statements can be read independently but neither explicitly says both are present. Count, separate use, and damage would need explicit rules; this differs from the selected initial one-facility policy.

A control change need not be equated with enemy-unit presence: Land §10.27 p. 18 recognizes mutually controlled hexes from opposing ZOCs, subject to its own definitions. Conversely, §24.81's presence wording is not identical to a change of exclusive control. Record control, occupying units, facility identity/type, and damage separately, with a defined check boundary and owner-relative meaning; do not infer a destruction result solely from a control event or a change in controller. Source says temporary facilities **may** be destroyed by presence; whether that is automatic or discretionary requires interpretation. Construction and resource rules under abstraction remain separate gates. These scenarios are synthetic, not evidence that the Commonwealth can reach Tobruk within six turns.

## Proposed tests after decisions

Test initial one-temporary-facility planning profile, then separately exercise control change with no enemy occupant, enemy entry without selected control transition, both simultaneously, and subsequent re-entry; distinguish temporary and major destruction consequences under a chosen §24.81 interpretation. Assert no duplicate facility or automatic replacement without the chosen lifecycle; test prior damage and ownership on any replacement event. Test §24.85 placement and per-player cap, and §24.87's separate construction, rebuilding and dismantling cases; do not equate these with vehicle repair eligibility. Independently verify major versus temporary repair column and weather, city-specific ZOC exception, availability and phase prerequisites, hidden-state-safe events, immutable ruleset identity, and replay. Tests are proposals, not executed outcomes.
