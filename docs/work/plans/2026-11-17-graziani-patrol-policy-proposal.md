# Patrol timing: bounded policy proposal

**Status: selected for planning in [RUL-GRA-19](2026-11-12-graziani-ruling-decisions.md#rul-gra-19-patrol-history-bounded-planning-interpretation), not admitted for runtime.** `gra-patrol-history-v1-proposed` evaluates each patrol at its own phase using authoritative events already completed in the current Operations Stage. It selects a stage-wide combat restriction and a current-stage target bombardment restriction. These choices are interpretations, not official corrections; changing them can alter available actions and intelligence. Historical reachability and the rest of patrol adjudication remain unproved.

The [independent policy review](2026-11-17-graziani-patrol-policy-review.md) prompted the causal-closure, Land-only supply and downstream-privacy safeguards below; independent admission remains outstanding. The [policy re-review](2026-11-17-graziani-patrol-policy-rereview.md) prompted the explicit distinction between predicate dominance and causal closure.

## Evidence and choice

Land rules [PDF/printed pp. 12 and 26](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), §§5.2, 6.14, 16.0 and 16.21–.22, were visually checked in the [timing source note](2026-11-17-graziani-rules-patrol-timing.md) and separately in its [review](2026-11-17-graziani-rules-patrol-timing-review.md). The same local Land scan was rehashed for this proposal: `b362870368b9fb8abe6918195fdcf878d4a8041c557b835b9a1bdde3c8b76e99`; September errata `5db539e1fac4aea3ef152370fb732f1938307fe4b2d47ad93eb605db0a09f0fb`. These are the same scans, not independent editions or rights grants.

Section 16.0 uses a stage-wide past occurrence formulation, whereas §16.21 refers to the phasing player engaging in anti-armor or close assault against enemy units. Section 5.2 orders A's patrol before B's own later movement/combat; both may participate in A's combat. The proposal gives operative weight to §16.0's broad wording and applies it to events through the actual patrol boundary, not future actions. This is conservative as to already completed combat, but does not claim §16.21 necessarily has that meaning or that the source rules exclude a whole-stage forward constraint. The target bombardment window uses §16.21's immediately preceding Operations Stage context, though its target sentence does not state a reset. Land §16.16 charges detailed-game patrol supplies; §32.25 p. 43 expressly waives patrol supply expenditure in the Land-only abstraction.

## Proposed history predicates

Use unique `(game turn, Operations Stage)` and a monotonic event position. At a player's phase-L patrol boundary, the authoritative history slice includes **completed** events from the start of that same stage strictly before the patrol command's adjudication position. It includes both players' portions so far, and excludes earlier stages, later commands and cancelled or declared-but-not-executed events. Evaluate every patrol request anew against the current slice and target, using the pre-command state. A resolved patrol and its consequences remain committed if a later player conducts combat; do not constrain that later combat under this proposal. Stage change starts a new slice but must not erase durable prior events. A stage/phase completion watermark or equivalent causal proof must establish that all potentially qualifying earlier actions and their events are complete through the boundary, including asynchronous air and combat work. For a predicate with no verified disqualifying occurrence, pending, partially executed, missing or ambiguously classified relevant actions prevent a known pass; only a proven cancellation without occurrence can be excluded. A verified disqualifying occurrence establishes that predicate as known failure even if another event is unresolved. This internal logical result does not waive the separate causal-closure gate below. Late completions and corrections need an explicit versioned correction/replay protocol and cannot silently rewrite an already committed patrol.

1. If any qualifying anti-armor combat or close assault has actually occurred anywhere in that slice, patrol is ineligible for either side, regardless of initiator, participant, or target. A known absence allows this predicate; incomplete event coverage yields **unknown**, not absence. Classify actual occurrences using separately reviewed combat event semantics: a declared, cancelled, evaded, or unresolved attack is not automatically a completed qualifying event, and whether zero-effect fire counts is a separate admission gate.
2. For a proposed target hex, if any artillery barrage or air bombardment by either side actually occurred **at that hex** in the same slice, target is ineligible. Occupant changes do not reset this hex history. A bombardment of another hex does not by itself fail this target predicate. An older-stage attack does not fail it under this selected policy. What constitutes a completed barrage or bombardment, including zero-effect or failed attacks, requires separately reviewed event semantics. Missing history or unresolved target identity yields **unknown**.
3. For each predicate, a verified disqualifying witness yields known failure; without one, complete classified coverage yields pass and incomplete coverage yields unknown. A known failure dominates another predicate's unknown in the internal conjunction. Separately, require causal closure through the current patrol cut before committing an action **or an externally observable eligibility decision**, even when an internal failure is known. This closure concerns the current cut, not B's later actions. Both predicates must be known and pass to proceed. This decision alone never proves a patrol legal: all other requirements, including §16.11 point types and errata, §16.17 weather, §16.22 target occupancy and path, profile-specific supply rules, losses, disclosure and ruleset-specific timing must also be decided. An unsupported prerequisite must not be converted into a legal action. For Graziani Land-only, §32.25 requires no patrol supply expenditure: do not impose a generic ammo or fuel availability gate from detailed §16.16. CP and point eligibility remain separate. This proposal does not define the interaction of air-phase scheduling with patrol eligibility; admission requires complete stage-scoped air-event coverage.

The choice is deliberately global, not a test of only the specific patrol unit or the phasing side's own attacks. With no earlier qualifying combat, A may patrol, then B may attack. If A has already conducted qualifying combat, B cannot patrol later in the same stage even if B did not initiate it. Barrage elsewhere alone does not trigger the combat predicate. Current-stage fire against the proposed target blocks that target even if the other player fired it. These are chosen outcomes, not quoted source examples.

## Alternatives and admission gates

An **initiator-only** reading applies §16.21 to attacks initiated by the phasing side; a **participant** reading includes defense and requires defined participant scope. Both can permit a later B patrol that this proposal denies. A **whole-stage** reading would need a forward restriction, delayed patrol or retrospective mechanism; no such mechanism is established by the cited passage. An **unbounded target history** reads `has undergone` without a reset and can block a previously bombarded hex in later stages or turns; the selected stage window is not explicit source text. Retain full event history so any accepted later version can reproduce its own decision. Version all decisions; do not silently reinterpret existing saves.

Unknown inputs must remain distinct from known ineligibility internally. Do not expose specific hidden combat, bombardment, target state, event coverage, or uncertainty through reason strings, legal-action enumeration, timing, or differences between pending and rejected responses. Side-safe command availability and publication timing need an explicit observable-equivalence contract: common messages alone do not prevent a leak. Privacy review must also cover resulting observations, RNG draws, casualties, event counts, subsequent state, and history correction. A successful patrol may produce disclosures the audience is authorized to observe; the contract must identify those entitlements. Different hidden histories that cause execution versus refusal can produce distinguishable later play despite identical immediate messages. Adopt an authorized disclosure or a complete public capability gate or other protocol before admission. If safe response or event coverage cannot be established, keep the patrol capability gated rather than emit a revealing result.

Before admission, independently review this selection and original sources, define exact completed-event semantics and phase ordering including air actions, capture authoritative event position, role and causal completion watermark, define correction and replay semantics, bind identity and weather/terrain proofs, define audience-safe responses and timing, and pin ruleset and event-schema versions. Snapshot and replay must preserve the stage key, full relevant event facts and the evaluated policy version; rehydration must not infer missing facts as negative. No implementation or readiness change follows this proposal.

## Synthetic validation matrix

After policy review and implementation: test A patrol followed by B combat and reverse initiative; A combat followed by B patrol for each combat type, including B's defensive participation; another-hex and target bombardment from each side; same target in previous stage and turn; vacated/reoccupied target; cancelled versus completed attack and zero-effect classification; boundary ordering at equal timestamps; earlier unresolved execution, late completion or correction, duplicate events and patrol-loss classification; stage rollover; missing combat or air history and unknown map identity; known disqualifier plus unresolved unrelated event, unknown alone and complete verified absence, checking both predicate dominance and the independent closure gate. Separately test weather, points, detailed-game supplies and the Land-only no-expenditure exception, occupancy, path and surviving reconnaissance result. Compare uninterrupted and replayed outcomes and audience-visible traces, including timing and action lists, across hidden-history variants. These are proposed tests, not executed evidence.
