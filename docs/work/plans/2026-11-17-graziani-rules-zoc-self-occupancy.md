# Enemy ZOC and a combat traveler's own presence

**Disposition:** inspected clauses contain no explicit example or definition resolving whether the moving combat unit itself supplies §10.26's friendly combat presence. Read literally, self-presence is possible; applying that interpretation to every effect creates tension with the printed contact, stop and break-off rules. A different-unit or effect-specific interpretation is plausible but adds a qualification. Do not admit either as source fact or silently change rail or ordinary movement.

## Source observations

[Land rules](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), PDF/printed pp. 13–15 and 17–18:

- §10.26 p. 18 says that for **all movement purposes** the presence of a friendly combat unit in a hex negates the *effect* of an enemy ZOC; it does not say “another,” specify a stationary protector, distinguish entry from departure, or state that the ZOC disappears. §10.27 says opposing units projecting ZOCs on each other are in each other's ZOC, and opposing ZOCs into the same hex mean both players control it. These clauses distinguish possible physical ZOC/control from its movement effects, but do not explicitly decide which effects §10.26 negates.
- §10.23 requires movement to cease on entering an enemy-controlled hex and, when starting a Movement Segment there, refers to the CP cost of breaking off. It prohibits retreat into enemy ZOC with an explicit §10.26 exception. §10.24 bars movement directly between enemy-controlled hexes, with a stated advance exception; §8.15 p. 14 similarly gives movement and break-off conditions. These passages do not explicitly say whether an existing separate combat occupant is required for the §10.26 retreat exception.
- §8.14 p. 14 says a unit entering enemy ZOC stops immediately. §8.24 says a phasing unit beginning a Movement/Combat Phase in an enemy ZOC pays two CP to break Contact before moving, or four for an Engaged result; the latter applies regardless of enemy ZOC. §8.62–.65 p. 15 define Contact by being in enemy ZOC at the beginning of a Movement Segment and specify break-off costs. The §8.24 phase versus §8.62 segment wording itself needs reconciliation. Section 8.68 limits contact/engaged markers to combat units in the hex at placement; it does not settle the self-presence reading.
- §10.29 p. 18 says a truck convoy may not enter an enemy ZOC unless the hex is **already** occupied by a friendly combat unit. This explicitly supplies a pre-existing occupant for convoy entry, not an express rule that every other kind of moving unit needs a *different* occupant. §10.21 restricts projection through certain terrain and into terrain the projecting unit could not enter. Section 8.0 p. 13 permits movement one unit at a time or in stacks, so the meaning of a distinct, already present unit and formation representation needs specification.
- §8.73 p. 15 separately bars a rail traveler beginning in an enemy ZOC or entering enemy ZOC or an enemy-occupied hex. Whether §10.26 applies to rail and whether a contact break-off CP could coexist with §8.73's stage-wide CP condition are separate decisions; no reconciliation is printed in the cited clauses. Section 8.77 p. 16 allows pickups/dropoffs under stated restrictions.

[September 1979 errata](https://www.spigames.net/db_pages/ERR_CampaignforNorthAfrica.pdf) PDF p. 1 corrects §8.73 by barring reorganization-point earnings during a rail-travel stage; its §10.3 entry concerns the non-phasing player. No explicit §8.14–.15, §8.24, §8.6 or §10.23–.29 clarification was found while inspecting the four-page package. This bounded negative search does not exclude other source passages, later errata or authoritative clarification.

## Competing readings

1. **Self counts broadly.** A friendly combat traveler is present in its origin, so §10.26 negates movement effects there without another unit. At entry, deciding whether it may count itself before entry is legal creates circularity. If self-presence also erases contact and stop effects after arrival, ordinary combat-unit ZOC restrictions can become ineffective; interpreting those restrictions as distinct from the negated effect instead requires a defensible effect-specific boundary. The wording does not provide that boundary expressly.
2. **A separate protector counts.** Treat the friendly combat unit as distinct from the moving subject, already present when the relevant entry or departure is evaluated. This preserves operational meaning for contact and is compatible with §10.29's convoy wording, but §10.26 itself does not say “other” or “already”; convoy-specific language cannot silently rewrite general movement. Decide whether another moving member of the same stack qualifies, and how an attached formation is represented.
3. **Effect-specific hierarchy.** Apply §10.26 to transit/retreat into a hex protected by friendly combat units while enforcing explicit stop, contact and break-off clauses on a unit already there. This may reconcile practical consequences but the selected boundaries and source precedence are project interpretations, not an explicit definition in the inspected text.

The [rail policy proposal](2026-11-17-graziani-rail-zoc-policy-proposal.md) selects self at origin for planning, but its [independent review](2026-11-17-graziani-rail-zoc-policy-review.md) defers admission over precisely this ordinary movement/contact tension. No result here resolves it. Before selecting a rule, define origin versus pre-entry occupancy, stack batching, physical ZOC versus movement effects, contact status and break-off timing, and relation to §8.73; preserve full alternatives and hidden-state-safe behavior.

## Distinguishing synthetic checks

For the same certified enemy ZOC, compare a lone combat unit starting there, the same traveler with a separately present friendly combat unit, a non-combat traveler with and without that protector, and movement into a protected versus unprotected ZOC. Exercise stop, direct ZOC-to-ZOC move, Contact marker and break-off cost, retreat destination and rail origin separately. Include a stack whose members move together, plus incomplete occupant or incoming-ZOC evidence; do not infer absence from incomplete coverage. Compare source-specific convoy pre-existence rules independently. These are questions for future accepted policy tests, not executed results or claims of historical reachability.
