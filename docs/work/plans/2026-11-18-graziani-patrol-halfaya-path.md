# Halfaya patrol candidate: two-edge path

**Result:** The proposed C3922 → C3921 → C3920 route is geometrically two adjacent edges, within §16.22's five-hex limit, but is **not an established legal patrol path**. Its first edge crosses the escarpment from the lower to the higher side. An actual motorized-infantry patrol's treatment under the abstract patrol terrain test must be resolved before declaring that crossing passable or forbidden. The target-unit ZOC exception concerns a different restriction and does not remove impassable terrain.

Land §10.21(b) prevents ZOC projection **through** the C3921/C3922 escarpment in either direction, regardless of whether some movement could cross it. That concerns projection through the first edge; it neither prevents the target at C3920 from potentially projecting across the *other* C3920/C3921 side nor decides whether the first edge is passable for a patrol. Other enemy units and their ZOCs require their own state and side evidence. The path's final enemy-occupied hex is its stated objective; do not infer a general permission to traverse unrelated occupied hexes from that target requirement.

A bounded discriminator after accepting map identities and applicable patrol-unit semantics is the exact directed route above with a qualified C3920 target, C3921 in that target's own ZOC, and known occupancy elsewhere. Test the first edge's vehicle restriction independently from the intermediate ZOC exception and unknown boundary effect. Compare a separately sourced alternative path only after establishing every edge and intermediate state; this two-edge check does not rule out another route. No actual action, patrol loss, disclosure or die roll is asserted.
