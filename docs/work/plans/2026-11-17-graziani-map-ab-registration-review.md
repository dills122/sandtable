## Evidence limit

Any two similarly sized, similarly oriented regular cells can be made to coincide closely by translation. Since all four measured vertices belong to one candidate cell and translation is fitted using those same points, low residual checks shape consistency and picking but supplies no discriminating test of which cell is the intended counterpart. Even multiple generic grid vertices across a regular lattice, or held-out grid vertices, can improve estimated geometric transform and detect distortion while still leaving whole-cell lattice shifts ambiguous. A stronger alignment requires independently identified, distinctive correspondences and comparison against plausible competing lattice shifts, plus uncertainty and out-of-sample checks; imperfectly matching town and line marks must not be forced into exact agreement.

The shared El Abiar name, nearby grid-label sequences and local motif independently support a *locality* lead, with numbers obscured and features redrawn; they do not prove physical cell identity, official seam semantics or legal route continuity. The observed town-mark offset cautions against exact overlay but alone cannot prove different locations or a particular correct translation.
