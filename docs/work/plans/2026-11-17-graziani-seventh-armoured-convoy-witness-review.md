## Calculation and bounds

Scenario §60.41 places 7th Armoured at D3612 and 1 RTR at C3520; its named support components are separately placed. The OA lists 6 RTR and 7 Hussars under 4th Brigade and 1 RTR and 8 Hussars under 7th, with the respective printed strengths below. Section 19.31 shows two armoured brigades and one support group for GT1–18 type-I armour, and three tank battalions per type-I armoured brigade. Under those source premises, 6 RTR and 7 Hussars at ten tanks apiece exceed half of code `g`'s ten-tank maximum, as do 1 RTR and 8 Hussars at seven apiece. With a three-battalion authorization and the stated qualifying/default attachments, 4th Armoured Brigade has 2/3, while 7th Armoured Brigade has 1/3 because 1 RTR is separately placed at C3520. If the support group contributes no qualifying brigade-equivalent at D3612, only one of three authorized brigade-equivalents counts; 1/3 is at or below the 50% division shell boundary. This conclusion depends on actual default attachments and on no legal setup alternative supplying another qualifying brigade. The support-group HQ alone is not a proven non-shell formation.

If 1 RTR later joins 8 Hussars with both qualifying and legally attached, 7th Brigade becomes 2/3. If 4th retains both qualifying battalions and both brigades are legally attached to the division, division count is 2/3, above 50%, so non-shell. This conclusion requires the applicable three-slot authorizations, strength and shell-exclusion rules and actual attachments at the same snapshot. It does not require treating the support group as non-shell. Moving one of 4th's two battalions to 7th leaves the tank-brigade counts at 1 and 2, hence only one qualifying tank brigade, assuming those ordinary three-slot rules. It does not establish whether reassignment is permitted or what other units can contribute. Do not generalize the two-of-three test to the separate two-battalion exception: §9.26 requires two attached battalions, at least one of which is non-shell, for a brigade with a maximum of two.

The distinction between assignment and actual attachment is essential. Same-hex setup organization, permitted substitutions, attachment costs and limits, timing, unit strength, terrain and stacking all remain independent checks. The candidate appropriately leaves route, timing, supply, enemy state and legal hierarchy unresolved; no straight-line index difference establishes reachable movement. The candidate's conditional 12→10 and 15→10 calculations total seven index changes, given its explicit transform, pair and threshold assumptions; they do not prove seven movement points or a global lower bound over all possible divisions and organizations. Actual convoy, qualifying order statistic, lane, attack and loss policy remain separate gates.
