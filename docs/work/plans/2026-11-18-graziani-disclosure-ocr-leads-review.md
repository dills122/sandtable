# Review: OCR-indexed disclosure leads

**Disposition:** Both identified original passages and the stated timing tension are supported. No material source error found. Their statements are conditional in-play procedures; neither determines a digital audience, a binding attack plan or an actual scenario action.

- [Land §15.22](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), p. 23, has the **phasing player** announce assaulted hexes; both players determine their respective total Points; then the phasing player states whether the attack is a Close Assault or Probe before individual assaults resolve sequentially. Determining totals does not itself state that all component identity, weapons or hidden composition are disclosed.
- Land §15.94, p. 25, says the probing player **does not have to announce** a probe until after the assault is over. The [§5.2 sequence](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), p. 12, says A need not reveal the probe decision during force assignment and announces which assaults were probes after each resolves. The candidate correctly identifies the tension with §15.22 without choosing source precedence or claiming the delayed case prohibits an earlier voluntary announcement.
- Land §8.53(b), p. 15, conditions the Reaction restriction on an adjacent moving enemy unit having a CPA **six or more points greater** than the non-phasing unit **and** the phasing unit's owner announcing that unit will Close Assault the non-phasing unit. This is a specific movement/reaction announcement, not proof every announced attack occurs, every plan is exposed or the later probe timing is settled. A two-player interaction does not by itself define all remote, teammate or electronic audiences.
- The candidate's treatment of §9.22 as a counter-characteristic description rather than a new blanket disclosure trigger is reasonable within this scope; I did not independently re-audit every OCR hit or the errata search. The existing partial §3.62 disclosures and source-specific exceptions still apply as written.
