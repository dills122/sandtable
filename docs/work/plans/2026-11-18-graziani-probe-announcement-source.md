# Probe announcement timing: source conflict

**Result:** The printed Land sequence and §15.94 support withholding whether an assault is a Probe until after resolution; §15.22 says the phasing player states that distinction before resolving individual assaults. The inspected September errata entries around §15.2 and §15.9 do not explicitly reconcile those timings. No source-backed precedence between the printed passages was found in this bounded check.

| Original passage | Timing and scope |
| --- | --- |
| [Land rules](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf) p. 12 §5.2 III.G.3(d) | At Force Assignment both players secretly assign TOE Strength Points; Player A determines probes but need not reveal that decision, and determines points withheld from assault. This describes a private assignment before Anti-Armor. |
| Same p. 12 §5.2 III.G.3(e)–(f) | Anti-Armor is resolved first. In the Close Assault Step Player A resolves assaults in chosen order and announces actual probes **after each is resolved**. The sequence explicitly uses Player A; no inference about every other disclosure follows. |
| Land p. 23 §15.22 | Phasing Player announces the hexes assaulted; both players determine their respective total Points. The phasing player states whether the attack is Close Assault or Probe, then individual assaults are resolved sequentially. This is a pre-resolution statement of the distinction and is in tension with the later permitted withholding. Target announcement and total determination do not by themselves establish unrestricted disclosure of unit composition. |
| Land p. 23 §15.25 and p. 25 §§15.91–.94 | Less than 50% of available TOE Strength Points committed to that or another attack makes the assault a Probe; §15.91 describes the same threshold. Section 15.94 says the probing player **does not have to announce** the Probe until after assault, while remembering assignment to Close Assault precedes Anti-Armor. This permission is different from an obligation to keep it secret until then. No actual target or threshold calculation is established here. |
| [September errata](https://www.spigames.net/db_pages/ERR_CampaignforNorthAfrica.pdf), pp. 1–2 | §15.25 example corrects the number of points withheld; §15.26 corrects case references; §15.27 corrects use of the same die roll for retreat/surrender. Nearby subsequent entries cover §§15.4, .53, .55, .56, .79 and .88; no §15.9 timing entry appears in these visually inspected numbered corrections. | These corrections do not expressly choose between the cited announcements. Text search for §15.22, §15.94 and Probe likewise found no direct entry; it cannot rule out indirect, OCR-missed, later or external clarification. |

The conflict is material when a phasing player chooses to withhold the distinction: §15.22's pre-resolution statement and §15.94's post-assault permission cannot both be applied literally at that moment. The sequence supplies a concrete delayed-announcement reading, but its existence alone is not an explicit publisher hierarchy overriding §15.22. Conversely, a general early statement cannot erase the express permission without an interpretation. Minimum discriminator: an official timing clarification identifying whether §15.22's statement is required before each assault or §15.94/§5.2 permits withholding its disclosure until afterward; absent that, record a project interpretation and its disclosure consequences explicitly. This is a source conflict, not a live action or policy decision.
