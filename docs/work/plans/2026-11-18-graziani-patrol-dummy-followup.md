# Graziani Land-only patrol: dummy-only target boundary

**Date:** November 18, 2026. Follow-up to the [patrol interaction note](2026-11-18-graziani-patrol-interaction-followup.md). A dummy-only patrol target is not established as reachable or legal by these rules alone. The source expressly gives dummies a deception role, but does not unambiguously resolve their treatment at every patrol gate. This is source analysis, not a selected implementation or historical occurrence.

## Source-proven facts

- §16.22 requires an **enemy-occupied** target within five hexes and a qualifying path; §16.17 bars storm-affected origin or target. These are independent of the later report procedure. Section 16.42 places dummy tank battalions in a hex during Construction, but says they have no actual counters. Section 3.21 nevertheless lists Dummy Tank Formations among unit categories and repeats their lack of counters. These facts establish a location and a category, not by themselves a definition of enemy occupancy for a hex containing only a dummy.
- §16.41 says dummies are equivalent to tank battalions in size, worth nothing in combat, and have CPA zero. §16.45 describes them as worthless alone in a hex and permits the defender, when revealing information during reconnaissance of a hex with a dummy, to present an apparent tank battalion with a chosen designation and TOE strength. It says the patrol cannot know the deception until attacking. The case does not expressly guarantee a positive reconnaissance result, decide target eligibility, or specify exactly how a dummy counts toward §16.7's report quota.
- §3.1's combat-unit definition expressly says HQs, engineers and Dummy Tank Formations are considered combat units **in certain situations**. Section 3.23 says certain non-combat units may count as combat units for particular determinations, naming reconnaissance among examples, without explicitly assigning that treatment to dummies at §16.32. For that specific check §16.32 explicitly excludes AA/Flak and engineers but does not expressly list dummies. Consequently neither “worth nothing in combat” nor the list of two exclusions alone establishes the dummy's combat-unit classification for that check.
- If §16.32's no-combat condition is met, the defender must disclose that condition, the survival table is skipped and the patrol takes no survival loss; the rule does not say to skip the later reconnaissance and objective-loss procedures. §§16.51–.55 and §16.45 govern information subject to their own conditions. Section 16.34's objective loss chooses a combat-unit TOE loss, or, if none, lists trucks, AA/Flak, engineers and HQ in order. It does not expressly provide a dummy-only loss recipient. Section 16.46 instead describes dummy elimination by specified close-assault and anti-armor situations, not a patrol-table result.

## Exact unresolved interactions

1. **Target existence and choice.** If the dummy alone is treated as enemy occupancy, §16.22 may permit a target subject to the remaining gates; if only an actual qualifying counter or different definition satisfies occupancy, it may not. A dummy has no actual counter (§16.42), but §3.21 names it a unit and §16.45 explicitly describes it alone in a hex. Neither “no counter” nor “worthless alone” alone proves either proposed occupancy result. Establish current dummy location, existence and visibility separately from opponent knowledge; do not expose hidden state merely to make the target-choice decision.
2. **No-combat disclosure versus deception.** If a dummy-only target is treated as having no combat unit for §16.32, the explicit disclosure can reveal that a subsequently reported apparent tank battalion is not a real combat unit; this sits in tension with §16.45's assertion that the patroller cannot know the dummy before attacking. Alternatively, treating the dummy as combat for this specific check avoids that particular disclosure but subjects the patrol to survival losses; §§3.1 and 3.23 make context-sensitive classifications plausible without expressly deciding this one. A policy must reconcile the exact statements rather than silently suppress an obligatory disclosure or pretend the tension does not exist.
3. **Reports and losses.** Even if target eligibility is accepted, actual reconnaissance count, defender choice, phasing-player exclusions and the dummy's report-quota role need interpretation. For a dummy-only target, a positive objective-loss result also needs a specified eligible recipient or disposition: the listed §16.34 fallback does not name dummies. This is separate from the question whether §16.32 skips survival. Do not manufacture a real TOE point, prisoner, or guaranteed report from an apparent tank designation.
4. **Land-only availability.** Section 16.42 normally requires ten Stores in the placement hex. Section 32.0/.11 replaces ordinary supply expenditures, while §32.25 waives patrol expenditure. Whether dummy placement's Stores condition survives abstraction and whether the scenario actually supplies a dummy are separate admission and scenario-evidence questions. The exemption for patrol expenditure does not by itself create one. Section 16.43 limits each player to three dummies, at most one per hex, and prohibits their use by Italian formations, including mixed German formations; do not equate this with a proven scenario starting inventory.

For comparison after a ruling: dummy alone, dummy plus a real combat unit, AA/engineer-only, and a truly empty hex; distinguish authoritative occupancy, target eligibility, no-combat disclosure, survival draw, report, objective-loss recipient and player-visible information. These are unexecuted vectors, not prescribed outcomes.
