# Graziani victory city-hex scope

**Status:** source reading and policy alternatives for review; no adopted ruling,
map admission, route contract, or gameplay result. This check concerns §60.81's
city references, not the separate map-D quantifier or convoy procedure. See
[independent review](2026-11-17-graziani-victory-city-hex-scope-review.md).

## Source evidence

- [Scenario booklet](https://spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_Scenarios_DupFromAirRules.pdf),
  PDF p. 4 / printed p. 28, §60.81: Italian strategic victory requires taking
  and holding **either Alexandria or Cairo (any hex)** with a combat unit that
  can be supplied by convoy from any hex on Game-Map D. The parenthesis follows
  the two city names together; the source does not separately print a parenthesis
  after each name. Commonwealth tactical victory requires the listed locations
  while tracing supply by truck convoy back to Alexandria or Cairo; its decisive
  and strategic rows say supply as above. Those Commonwealth references do not
  explicitly say “any hex.”
- [Air/Logistics package](https://spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_AirGameRules.pdf),
  PDF p. 26, Summary of Important Locations: Alexandria lists E3613 and E3714;
  Cairo lists E1730, E1829, E1830, E1930 and E1931. Both have Major City and
  Victory Condition marks. These are source location candidates, not certified
  playable map membership, topology, or control.
- Same package, PDF p. 25, §37.42 Land Distance Chart: Alexandria E3613 and
  Cairo E1829 each carry an asterisk. Its footnote says **“One major city hex.”**
  The chart describes shortest hex distances and can cut across the Mediterranean.
  Its single representative per city is a distance-chart convention; it does
  not say those two hexes are exclusive victory objectives or supply endpoints,
  nor establish a legal convoy route.

These scans are same-edition source material, not independent editions or an
errata ruling. The scenario page also describes Alexandria airfields at both
E3613 and E3714, but an airfield listing does not itself define victory or
supply eligibility.

## Reading and choices

1. **Objective quantifier.** “Either” supports selecting one city; “any hex”
   supports one qualifying hex within the selected city rather than occupying
   every listed hex. Applying that reading to both named cities is an
   interpretation: the parenthesis appears after Cairo.
   A stricter all-hex possession rule or a narrower Cairo-only parenthesis is a
   distinct policy alternative, not established by the chart or summary.
2. **Unit binding.** The text ties taking and holding the selected city hex to
   **a combat unit that can be supplied**. The strongest direct reading uses
   the same unit at the selected objective hex as the supply-capability witness.
   That binding is an interpretation, not an explicit formal identity or temporal
   rule. An independent unit able to be supplied at another hex, or separate
   holding and supply-capability witnesses, is a more permissive alternative
   needing an explicit ruling; capability at another city hex does not establish
   capability for the holding unit. Taking, holding, substitution over time,
   unit qualification and evaluation timing remain separate decisions.
3. **Commonwealth city sources.** Alexandria or Cairo identifies a city, but the
   Commonwealth row does not say which hex of a multi-hex city terminates the
   trace. Plausible policies include any eligible hex in either city; the chart's
   representative hex only; or another specifically designated source hex.
   Source eligibility, control, and whether each objective can choose a different
   city or hex need explicit decisions. The chart footnote alone does not select
   one. Do not transfer Italian “any hex” automatically to this different clause.

For a future policy test, contrast a combat unit on a candidate city hex that
can be supplied under the applicable convoy predicate with a holder that cannot
be supplied under that predicate plus a unit on another city hex that can. Also
contrast a route to a non-chart city hex with one to E3613/E1829. Actual supply
status is a separate question. Expected outcomes depend on the accepted policy
and certified map; these are proposed cases, not results.
