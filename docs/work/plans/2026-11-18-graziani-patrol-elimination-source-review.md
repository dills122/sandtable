# Patrol elimination source check: review

**Disposition:** No material source error found in the [objective-loss check](2026-11-18-graziani-patrol-elimination-source.md). The explicit complete-elimination branch is strong evidence for an objective-loss roll, while §16.33's “goes no further” leaves the stated wording tension; neither passage explicitly supplies a precedence rule.

Visually checked Land p. 26: §16.31 requires both sides to check possible losses; §16.33 says no surviving Patrol Points means the phasing player goes no further and gets no information. Section 16.34 explicitly provides the objective-loss roll after obtaining information or all units are eliminated, whichever comes first, regardless of own losses; it changes the captured result to eliminated if the patrol is entirely eliminated. Section 16.32's no-combat-units branch skips the survival table and patrol loss, with AA/Flak and engineers excluded for that specific combat-unit test. None of these words expressly says whether captured-only or mixed captured/killed no-survivor results equal complete elimination.

Common chart p. 3 §16.8 prints killed/captured `0/0` on 1–4, `0/1*` on 5 and `1/0` on 6; its star says a completely eliminated patrolling force changes the result to one TOE Strength Point killed. The proposed all-killed, die-5 comparison correctly distinguishes no objective roll from a roll yielding one killed point under the other reading, subject to eligible objective-loss allocation and other conditions. It is a conditional discriminator, not an actual legal witness.

September errata's §16.11 adds eligible patrol types; the targeted text search does not supply a directly numbered §16.33/.34 priority correction. This bounded result cannot exclude an indirect or later ruling. The procedure's final objective-loss sentence and chart support continuing after elimination, but their interaction with §16.33 is an interpretation rather than an explicit hierarchy.
