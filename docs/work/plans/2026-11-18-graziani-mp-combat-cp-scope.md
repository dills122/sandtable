# §32.58(C): motorized supply and combat CP

**Finding:** Land §11.25's hex-wide combat CP charge is the concrete interaction with §32.58(C). The former applies combat CP expenditures to all units in the hex even if some do not participate; the latter says Motorization Points **transporting a Supply Unit** need not expend CP if stacked with combat units participating in combat. The exemption is evidence for those qualified points, not for their combat escort, every supply counter, or all CP expenditures throughout a stage. Whether an abstract point allocation is itself one of §11.25's “units,” precisely when its condition holds, and how a represented carrier/cargo is accounted for require an explicit reading; the text does not supply a complete transaction model.

## Exact charges and order

- Land §11.21, p. 19, describes non-phasing combat expenditure, normally **3 CP**, including a barrage it fires, specified assaults it undergoes (Probe **2 CP**) and holding-off barrage; §11.27 has a **1 CP** exception for a defender in a full assault with final differential minus four or worse. §§11.22–.24 give a phasing unit **5 CP** for barrage or anti-armor/close assault, **2 CP** for probing and **3 CP** for undergoing barrage, with per-Combat-Segment caps of five phasing and three non-phasing. Do not apply every charge cumulatively or assume the 1-CP exception in an arbitrary fight.
- §11.25 expressly extends CP expenditure to **all units in a hex**, including those not participating in any way, for phasing and non-phasing units; its examples concern combat units or a formation. §32.58(C), p. 44, names Motorization Points transporting a Supply Unit and conditions the exception on stacking with combat units **participating in combat**. These phrases identify the particular overlap; neither says all stacked combat units are exempt, nor defines whether an unrepresented allocation is a separate §11.25 unit. If a qualifying carrier is subject to that general hex charge, §32.58(C) is the specific source for not requiring its CP expenditure under its stated condition; precise interpretation and attribution remain open.
- §5.2 III.G, p. 12, repeats Movement, Breakdown, Combat and Reserve Release segments. Its Combat Segment orders position determination, barrage, retreat before assault, force assignment, anti-armor and close assault. §6.14 tracks CP across both players' portions of the Operations Stage. §6.14's example explicitly distinguishes a threatened close assault from one executed after the defender retreats. A stack and its participating unit may change over that sequence; the printed exception does not specify an instantaneous checkpoint, whether an announced attack alone suffices, or how to handle a unit leaving the hex before a later combat step.
- **Retreat and other costs remain distinct:** §§13.1 and 13.21, p. 21, describe eligible non-phasing retreat before assault as voluntary movement expending CP and fuel for vehicles; whether the supply carrier qualifies as a retreating subject and how §32.58(C) relates to an actual retreat require separate interpretation. §32.17, p. 43, charges half the CPA of all units in a hex for an attempted Supply Unit destruction before movement in a Movement/Combat subsegment; its actor and payer semantics are already disputed. Neither a general waiver of this destruction charge nor an exemption from movement CP or fuel follows simply from combat co-location. See [carrier classification review](2026-11-18-graziani-carrier-retreat-classification-review.md) and [destruction cost review](2026-11-17-graziani-destruction-cpa-basis.md).
- **Transport condition:** §32.32 requires 30 points for a real Supply Unit and six for a dummy, with attachment/detachment during Organization; a unit lacking its minimum may not move. §32.33 lets a transported Supply Unit move in the owner's Movement/Combat or Truck Convoy Phase, not both, and requires it to begin and remain stacked with a friendly combat unit for the former, while excluding normal non-phasing movement from that restriction. That escort restriction does not establish that the escort actually participates in combat, and a stationary Supply Unit's mere co-location does not establish assigned transporting points. §32.24 separately states the real-supply transporter fuel cost; its grouping and actual movement are independent questions.

## Conditional Graziani candidate

Scenario §60.41, PDF p. 3 / printed p. 27, lists Matruh Garrison with **1 Essex** attached at D3714; §60.92(C), PDF p. 5 / printed p. 29, lists five regular Supply Units there. Separately §60.43 lists 40 medium and five heavy second/third-line truck points for distribution anywhere on the maps. §§59.44, 59.63(B) and 59.66(C), PDF p. 2, supply a conditional route to a same-hex group of 30 eligible unattached Motorization Points starting with an **additional** regular Supply Unit; that does not establish the source-listed five are already carried. The [Matruh review](2026-11-18-graziani-draw-setup-witness-review.md) supports the named co-location while reserving formation and stacking interpretation.

This is a **candidate for further construction, not a legal or reachable combat witness**. A proposed state would need a lawful initial point distribution and optional extra load, supply stacking/capacity and priority, qualifying 1 Essex/parent representation, an actual same-hex combat participant and combat event, and a chosen phase snapshot. Five printed Supply Units and first-line trucks at Matruh alone prove no 30-point supply assignment. No enemy approach, legal attack, point expenditure, actual transport or six-turn action is established. Once the state and policy are separately admitted, compare qualified carrier points against the participating combat unit and an unqualified/non-transporting allocation; preserve combat CP and any movement/fuel expenditure as separately sourced events.
