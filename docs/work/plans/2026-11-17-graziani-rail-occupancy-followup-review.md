# Graziani rail occupancy: independent source review

**Disposition:** Defer admission. The follow-up correctly presents self-origin and pre-entry occupancy as provisional policy, not printed rail rulings. Its ordinary-movement warning is material: accepting self-presence generally could defeat rules expressly governing combat units already in enemy ZOC. Neither a rail-only exception nor a universal self-presence rule is established by these passages alone.

## Findings

1. **CP wording requires an explicit decision.** Land p. 15 §8.73 bars CP expenditure for another reason during the Operations Stage; p. 16 §8.77 says no unit using rail has expended CP in that stage. The latter does not repeat “for any other reason.” The follow-up notes the former and calls the condition stage-wide, but should also cite and reconcile §8.77, including whether its past-tense wording and §8.73 constrain later expenditure. Do not assume either a complete post-rail ban or permission to spend CP later without a ruling.
2. **Self-presence has observable ordinary-movement consequences.** Land p. 18 §10.23 requires a unit entering an enemy-controlled hex to stop and addresses departure in a subsequent Movement Segment; §10.24 restricts movement directly between enemy-controlled hexes. Land p. 15 §§8.62–.65 define Contact at the beginning of a Movement Segment and impose break-off costs. A literal application of §10.26 to a combat unit's own occupancy could undermine those effects, but §10.26 speaks of movement effects, not erasing ZOC or Contact markers. Resolve the interaction for each consequence, including entry timing, before choosing a general rule. Section 10.23 explicitly names §10.26 for retreat, evidence for its application there but not a complete timing rule for other movement.
3. **Snapshot wording needs an action contract.** Section 8.77 permits pickup and dropoff along the line, subject to stacking, direction and CP restrictions. Excluding every traveler in the current rail action under a pre-action snapshot is conservative policy, not a printed prohibition on using a previously dropped unit as presence. Conversely, evaluating state at each entry is underspecified until atomicity and pickup/dropoff ordering are defined. Keep the deferred gate; compare both interpretations with a dropped qualifying unit and a later traveler, without implying either sequence is source-proven legal.

## Source check

Visually inspected [Land rules](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), printed/PDF pp. 15–16 and 18, §§8.62–.78 and 10.16–.29, on November 17, 2026. Section 8.73 separately prohibits entry into enemy ZOC or an enemy-occupied hex; §10.26 negates the **effect** of enemy ZOC for all movement purposes when a friendly combat unit is present. Neither expressly specifies rail precedence, another-unit qualification, or pre-entry timing. Section 10.29's already-occupied condition directly concerns truck convoys; its separate non-combat restriction is not an express rail timing rule. Section 10.27 preserves mutual control, so movement-effect negation alone does not remove physical ZOC or control.

This review checked the cited scan, not an independent edition or publisher ruling. No runtime changes or tests were made; candidate vectors remain hypothetical and do not establish an actual legal Graziani route.
