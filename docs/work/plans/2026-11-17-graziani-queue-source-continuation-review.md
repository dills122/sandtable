# Independent review: Maaten Baggush road-side attribution

**Date:** November 17, 2026. **Disposition:** Candidate interior road crossing
is visually plausible, but D3419–D3420 road membership is not established by
this evidence. Retain unknown; do not amend source data on this reading alone.

The terrain key on PDF p. 1 labels two continuous parallel dark strokes as
road, distinct from the blue cross-tied railroad. In Map D p. 4, the 800-dpi
crop shows those parallel strokes in the Maaten Baggush area. The shared
D3419–D3420 vertical grid line begins at the crowded upper vertex near
`(326,480)`; the lower road stroke intersects it near `(326,525)`, while the
upper stroke merges into the vertex. The blue railway crosses the vertical
side farther below. The 500-dpi wider crop supplies neighboring printed cell
labels and grid context. These observations support the candidate's geometric
reading: if the road is represented by the midpoint between the visible borders,
its inferred intersection falls below the vertex, inside that side.

That midpoint is an interpretation, not an explicit line in the key or a
verified rule for assigning a road touching a three-hex junction. A different
source-defined alignment or junction rule could assign the adjacent diagonal,
or leave ownership indeterminate even though the lower border touches the
vertical side. These are **counterexamples to the inference that lower-border
contact alone proves ownership**, not independently observed alternative
crossings or evidence of an actual rule. No definite centerline crossing of
an adjacent side was established in this scan. Overprinting prevents isolating
the upper stroke at the vertex; a higher-resolution render of the same scan
does not provide independent artwork or settle the rule. The key establishes
symbol identity, not vertex ownership; the scan supplies geometry, not an
authoritative tie-break.
