# Victory control: contextual definitions in original rules

**Result:** Two further printed contexts give different descriptions of “control”: Land §30.56 ties port control to the last player to occupy its hex, while the separate Crusader scenario §62.8 defines city/village control by current combat-unit occupation. Neither passage expressly defines the Graziani §60.81 take/hold/retain predicates or overrides §10.27's shared-ZOC control. The terms therefore need case-specific handling; no victory rule is selected here.

## Source comparisons

- Land §10.0 general rule, printed/PDF p. 17, calls hexes upon which a unit exerts a ZOC **controlled hexes**. Section 10.27, p. 18, expressly allows a hex to be controlled by both players when opposing units exert ZOCs into it. [Prior source note](2026-11-18-graziani-victory-remaining-source.md) and [review](2026-11-18-graziani-victory-remaining-source-review.md) already establish the qualification and §10.26's movement-only neutralization.
- Land §30.56, p. 42, in §30.5 naval transport **of troops**, allows transfer between friendly-controlled ports and parenthetically states that control means **the last player to occupy the hex**. This is affirmative historical-occupation wording in that port-transfer context; the sentence does not specify a combat-unit requirement or expressly apply to every hex or victory condition. An unoccupied port last occupied by one side is a conditional comparison case if the last occupation and all other prerequisites are established; it does not imply port transfer succeeds or that a Graziani objective remains held after departure.
- Scenario §62.8, scenario PDF p. 9 / printed p. 33, is the victory section for **Operation Crusader**, a different scenario group. It defines control of a city/village as occupying it with a combat unit of any strength and also specifies supply requirements for that unit. The current-combat-occupancy wording is stronger than merely being the last player to occupy a hex. Its separate scenario and supply conditions are not a general correction to Graziani. Compare [Graziani §60.81](https://spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_Scenarios_DupFromAirRules.pdf), p. 28: its Italian decisive and tactical rows explicitly use take and hold **(occupy)** for the named objectives, while other predicates use hold or retain and their own supply language. Section 60.81 does not expressly cite §30.56 or §62.8 to define those terms.

These are context-dependent source formulations, not proof that their words always conflict in application or that any is universally authoritative. In a hypothetical hex with opposing projected ZOCs, no occupant, and a known last occupant, §10.27 can describe shared ZOC control while §30.56's port-transfer definition identifies the last occupier; this alone neither resolves the interaction where both rules could apply nor assigns a §60.81 outcome. Likewise §62.8's rule cannot be imported to settle same-snapshot exclusive possession for another scenario without an explicit interpretation. Terrain, unit classification, legal entry, starting control, timing, capture history, and qualifying supply remain separate gates. No coexistence, simultaneous victory or actual reachability is established.

The Land glossary p. 5 includes a Zone of Control entry, not a separately headed general `control`, `hold`, or `occupy` definition on that inspected page.
