# Graziani compulsory combat: eligibility and continuation

**Disposition:** In each applicable phasing Combat Segment, test the enemy's
ZOC projection onto phasing combat units; the source does not require those
phasing units themselves to project a ZOC. A qualifying obligation is not an
automatic assault, nor a license to end the segment when no assault is legal.
The rules offer exceptions, holding off and retreat, with unresolved interactions.
No historical contact or executable six-turn completion is established.

## Direction and scope

The [earlier obligation reading](2026-11-17-graziani-turn-obligation-followup.md)
already covers §10.31–.36, probe threshold, broad sequencing and privacy. This
continuation adds asymmetric ZOC, assault eligibility, and the competing
terrain-retreat text. Land §10.31 identifies **enemy-occupied source hexes**
whose units exert a ZOC on friendly combat units; September errata §10.3 limits
the requirement to the phasing player. Thus a qualifying non-phasing enemy
source can oblige the phasing side even when the phasing stack lacks the
strength, organization or cohesion to project its own ZOC. Reverse the
projection alone and §10.31 supplies no phasing obligation from that source.
Land §10.27 describes mutual ZOC only when opposing units actually exert ZOCs
on one another; it does not make projection reciprocal. §10.21's blocked
hexsides and entry restrictions still apply to the *enemy source's* projection.
A combat unit's mere adjacency, a friendly ZOC, or an enemy non-projecting
stack is not a substitute for this predicate.

Scenario §§60.22 and 60.6 set turn 1, stage 1 through turn 6, stage 3 and
Italian initiative in turn 1; Land §5.2 allows the initiative holder to pick
relative player order. Apply this directional test separately to each actual
phasing cycle, including an unmoving unit, after movement and reactions cease.
There are 18 stages with two player blocks, potentially multiple cycles per
block, not a fixed count of mandatory battles. Contact and a reachable eligible
source require certified state and player choices; neither follows from the
scenario dates.

## Choice and impossibility branches

- §10.32 exempts friendly units in the enemy ZOC when only artillery, anti-tank,
  anti-aircraft or non-combat units are present, or other types present are
  pinned. Pinning is target-specific under errata §12.44; §12.44 prevents
  pinned units moving or participating in anti-armor or close assault for the
  rest of that segment, with gun-class and artillery-HQ exceptions. Resolve
  mixed stacks and timing explicitly; don't treat a single pinned component as
  proof that every other eligible unit is exempt.
- §10.33–.35 permits holding-off barrage at §10.34's actual-point threshold;
  absent a sufficient holding off, §10.35 calls for close assault subject to
  §10.32, without a minimum assault point requirement. §12 requires a barrage
  rating and sufficient ammunition; forward/back position, allocation and
  targets matter. A threshold count alone is not evidence a legal barrage is
  available. The earlier reading records the remaining §10.31/holding-off
  interpretation; do not quietly treat any barrage as sufficient.
- Legal assault requires more than an adjacent counter. §§15.11, .13–.17 and
  .21, together with §12.44, govern ratings, forward/back guns, ammunition,
  pinning and allocation; §15.34 bars motorized assault up an escarpment or
  against salt-marsh defenders. §15.11 permits units without ratings to
  participate without contributing points, whereas §15.21 describes phasing
  units with offensive ratings; reconcile these texts before deciding whether
  a zero-rated or unrated participant alone can constitute an assault. Zero
  *minimum points* in §10.35 is not an exemption from eligibility or terrain.
  §15.29 says no phasing allocation means no assault and invokes §10.31 et seq;
  it does not authorize an invented automatic attacker or a free pass through
  an outstanding obligation. Defender non-allocation has its own three-hex
  retreat and disorganization result under §15.29; it is not the same case.
- §15.34 says a combat unit in enemy ZOC that cannot attack due to terrain must
  retreat **one** hex unless another unit attacks that enemy. §10.36 says a
  unit unable to assault or hold off for any reason retreats **three** hexes,
  paying movement CPs and receiving three extra disorganization points, with
  its own destination and forced-enemy-ZOC surrender language. These potentially
  overlapping prescriptions have different triggers, distances and exceptions.
  Preserve both; decide precedence and timing from further authority or an
  explicit versioned ruling before implementing. Do not generalize §15.34's
  other-unit exception to every §10.36 impossibility, or silently choose one
  retreat, surrender, or no-op when routes are unavailable.
- A candidate overlap witness uses a qualifying enemy infantry source in a
  salt-marsh hex and an adjacent phasing motorized combat unit on clear terrain.
  Certify that the enemy source can enter the clear hex from its position and
  satisfies §10.11–.15, that no blocked hexside prevents projection, that no
  other unit attacks that enemy, and that no legal holding-off fire exists.
  Section 15.34 bars that motorized unit's assault against salt-marsh defenders;
  subject to the remaining prerequisites, both retreat texts are implicated.
  Check all entry, terrain, ammunition and route restrictions in the fixture.
  By contrast, an escarpment hexside blocks ZOC under §10.21(b), so an enemy
  solely across that edge cannot establish this witness. Another source's ZOC
  does not automatically establish that §15.34 refers to the same enemy.
- §8.62 defines Contact at the start of a Movement Segment; §8.63 makes
  Engaged a close-assault result, not synonymous with ZOC. §§8.64–.68 define
  breaking off, costs and marker scope. Movement before combat can change the
  obligation, but Contact/Engaged markers alone do not prove current projection
  or authorize a fabricated combat choice. §15.93 excludes Contact and Engaged
  consequences for a probe, while §10.31 requires basic differential −4 or
  better for a probe to satisfy the obligation.

## Admission vectors

Use synthetic, otherwise legal and source-certified snapshots; none proves
historical reachability. Compare (1) enemy source projects onto phasing combat
unit that does not project back, versus only the phasing unit projects;
(2) same geometry with source strength or cohesion below threshold or an
impassable projection edge; (3) sole exempt types, individually pinned mixed
stack, and unpinned eligible component; (4) sufficient and insufficient legal
holding-off fire with ammunition and allocation checked; (5) out-of-ammunition,
pinned, back-gun, prohibited-terrain and no-rating candidates separately;
(6) the salt-marsh witness with §15.34's other attacking unit present versus
absent and §10.36 inability, with each proposed precedence policy explicitly
tested, plus the escarpment negative control; (7) no phasing assault
allocation versus defender non-allocation. Check legal choices, ensuing owner
choices, costs, effects and replay, including unresolved and unknown states.
No selectable eligible assault is not itself a new end-segment command or an
authorization to choose a retreat route on the player's behalf. Neither retreat
passage specifies a route-selection actor or algorithm; an owner route choice
and any deterministic fallback are proposed authority decisions, not source
facts. A fallback requires an independently accepted authority rule; missing
evidence remains unknown. Compare identical authorized observations with differing
hidden forces so legal options, error responses and subsequent behavior do not
reveal secrets beyond source-authorized disclosure.
