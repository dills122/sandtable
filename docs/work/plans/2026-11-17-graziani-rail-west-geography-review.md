# Independent review: Graziani rail west geography

**Disposition:** Geography note’s cautious conclusion is supported. D3615 is visibly west of D3616; this is a geographic example, not a proved §8.78 rail witness. Do not implement a general comparator from these sources alone. One wording clarification is recommended below.

## Findings

1. **Scope of rail qualification needs clarification.** The application boundary asks for “qualifying constructed nonhistorical rail.” Land §8.78 says “any rail hex occupied by an Axis combat unit”; it does not itself limit witnesses to nonhistorical rail. Section 8.76 separately says destroyed rail cannot be entered until repaired and rail not yet in existence cannot be used until constructed. If nonhistorical construction is specific to this candidate, say so explicitly; do not present it as a general §8.78 requirement. Neither geography nor §8.76 alone settles whether a destroyed occupied hex qualifies as a §8.78 witness.
2. **Boundary remains a policy choice.** Map D’s compass has west to the left, and the centers of labeled D3615 and D3616 are plainly ordered left to right, respectively, on the same `36xx` row. They are distinct neighboring printed hexes. This supports the cited local comparison without choosing whether “west” for arbitrary hexes compares centers, whole hex areas, a grid axis, or another directional boundary. Equal projected positions, staggered rows, and hexes intersecting a proposed boundary still need an explicit rule. Local ordering does not prove rail crosses their shared side.
3. **Distance is not registration.** Air/Logistics §37.42’s footnote says a hex is always 33 hexes from a same-numbered hex on an adjacent section, giving D4102 relative to C4102 and E4102. Its metric counts shortest distance excluding origin and including destination, including hypothetical sea hexes. This establishes neither direction by itself nor a 33-column coordinate translation, seam alias, adjacency, traversability, or rail use. Land §4.1’s A-to-E arrangement supports west-to-east sheet order; applying it to precise hex positions remains an inference, especially within overlap.
4. **Seam conflict is material.** Land §4.1 literally calls for a succeeding section’s `01xx` hexrow to overlay the preceding section’s `39xx` hexrow. The visible grid and local seam comparison use `xx01` and `xx33`/`xx34` in the relevant edge region. Sheet order alone cannot decide whether a near-seam pair is identical or strictly ordered. Require independently established registration or a documented bounded policy before such a comparison; preserve unknown where evidence does not settle it.

The note correctly separates geographic order from occupied rail status, movement use, snapshot, and actual reachability. Its off-route witness reading follows the unqualified “any rail hex” wording, subject to those unresolved rail and position questions. No absence claim follows from an incomplete witness search.
