# Independent review: ZOC self-occupancy

**Disposition:** Source comparison supports preserving the three competing interpretations. §10.26 does not expressly say the friendly combat unit must be a separate protector; neither does it define self-qualification or clearly reconcile the other movement restrictions. Broad self, separate protector and effect-specific hierarchy are project interpretations, not settled source facts. No basis here to admit rail or ordinary movement.

## Source verification and additions

Visually re-read [Land rules](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), PDF/printed pp. 13–15 and 17–18, including §§8.0, 8.14–.15, 8.24, 8.62–.68, 8.73, 10.0–10.29. - §10.26 says the presence of a friendly combat unit negates the **effect** of enemy ZOC **for all movement purposes**; it does not expressly say another unit or pre-existing occupant. §10.23 specifically cites §10.26 as a retreat exception; §10.24's distinct direct enemy-controlled-hex movement exception concerns an advance into a vacated enemy hex. The effect-specific model may make practical sense, but must explain how it respects the broad text as well as the separate restrictions. The candidate correctly identifies the tension rather than claiming either hierarchy is printed.
- §8.14 requires a unit to stop on entering enemy ZOC; §8.24 uses the beginning of a Movement/Combat **Phase** for its break-off condition, while §8.62 defines Contact at the beginning of a Movement **Segment**, and §8.65 gives Contact break-off a two-CP cost. §8.24 also specifies four CP for Engaged even absent enemy ZOC. If self-presence negates every effect at origin, address these clauses and marker timing explicitly. This tension does not itself prove that an already-present *different* unit is required.
- §10.29 expressly requires a friendly combat unit already present for truck-convoy entry. Its separate non-combat clause bars voluntary entry into an unoccupied enemy-ZOC hex; its capture clause adds its own requirements. Do not turn the convoy's protector wording into a general rule without a policy decision or flatten all non-combat cases into convoy cases.
- **Projection qualification:** before any self-occupancy comparison, certify that a ZOC exists. §10.11 restricts which units project based on size/stack and excludes convoys and unaccompanied HQs; §10.15 adds a minimum of ten raw defensive close-assault points, and §10.21 limits terrain projection. A merely adjacent unit or friendly combat unit is not automatically a ZOC projector. These are conditions for source-backed test inputs, not a correction to §10.26.
- **Disclosure qualification:** §10.16 requires the non-phasing player to disclose whether a potentially projecting unit actually exerts ZOC when a phasing unit starts a Movement Segment in, or moves into, that hex. A hidden-state-safe design must account for this positive source disclosure as well as avoiding unauthorized information leaks; the passage does not automatically establish the same disclosure for a separately named rail phase. Observation and timing need their own contract.

## Consequences for selection

At origin distinguish physical presence, Contact/Engaged state, effect and cost; at entry evaluate whether arrival may itself supply the protection and, if not, when a qualifying protector is measured. Stack movement and attached-unit representation can change whether there is an independently present combat counter; incomplete identity or incoming-projection evidence must stay unknown. For rail, independently reconcile §8.73's separate origin/entry and enemy-occupancy prohibitions with §10.26 and its stage-wide other-CP requirement. Do not carry an accepted local rail choice into ordinary movement, convoy, retreat, combat or construction without separate review.

Suggested synthetic comparisons: a source-certified projecting enemy, combat versus non-combat traveler, lone versus different friendly protector, stack/attachment cases, origin versus entry, stop/direct-move/break-off/retreat/rail, and incomplete projection or occupancy. Include §10.16's authorized disclosure in paired-audience tests. These are discrimination vectors, not claims of reachable setup or executed tests.
