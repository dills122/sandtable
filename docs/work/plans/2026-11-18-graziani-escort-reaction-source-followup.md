# Motorized supply: reaction versus retreat before assault

**Date:** November 18, 2026. This bounded follow-up adds §13.0's distinct non-phasing movement procedure to the [escort/reaction analysis](2026-11-17-graziani-escort-reaction-followup.md) and [review](2026-11-17-graziani-escort-reaction-followup-review.md). Those notes already preserve the §8.53/§32.58 reaction and escort conflicts. This source comparison does not select a hierarchy, prove that an abstract Supply Unit or its Motorization Points can take a particular action, or establish a reachable Graziani contact.

## Printed procedures and interaction

- §8.53(b) allows a non-phasing unit that cannot react under its stated six-CPA differential and mover's announced Close Assault condition potentially to retreat before assault under §13.0. Its triggering attack must be by that adjacent mover against the non-phasing unit, as the existing review explains. This is a reference to a separate opportunity, not proof that the carrier meets every §13 condition or may react under §32.58(D).
- §13.0 says Retreat Before Assault follows completed barrages and should not be confused with Reaction. §13.1 permits any non-phasing unit not pinned to retreat before assault, except one at cohesion −26 or worse. This broad unit wording does not expressly classify an abstract Supply Unit, its assigned Motorization Points, or a combined carrier/cargo counter. Section 32.33's exception for normal non-phasing movement is evidence against automatically imposing its friendly Movement/Combat escort condition, but does not itself establish the subject's identity or waive other restrictions.
- §13.21 calls Retreat Before Assault **voluntary** movement, handled as other movement, with CP and vehicle fuel/breakdown language. §13.23 allows a unit beginning that step adjacent to an enemy combat unit to spend as many CP retreating as desired; §13.24 limits a unit not starting adjacent to four CP or one hex, whichever greater. Section 32.58(A) gives Supply Unit-transporting Motorization Points CPA 15, and (B) says that if **forced** to exceed it for any reason they return to the last hex where they had not exceeded it. Whether voluntary movement beyond CPA falls within that forced-return condition is not established merely by a retreat's name; decide movement subject, other restrictions and meaning of “forced” separately. Section 32.55 excludes Motorization Points themselves from breakdown, not every eligible vehicle.
- §13.26 requires a retreating unit to stop upon entering an enemy ZOC and disallows direct retreat from one enemy ZOC to another, with inability to avoid that restriction barring the retreat. In contrast, §8.55 says reacting units may **never enter** an enemy ZOC. General §10.26's friendly-combat-unit movement exception and §10.29's Truck Convoy entry rule require separate attention; do not assume these two movement procedures have interchangeable ZOC outcomes. This observation alone does not resolve whether any abstract carrier may enter a particular hex.
- §32.58(D) is written as a **reaction** permission with an adjacent mover CPA 35 threshold; §13.1/.21–.26 do not state that same threshold. Whether and how the carrier-specific wording affects the separate retreat procedure remains an interpretation. Likewise, §8.53(a)'s unescorted Truck Convoy bar expressly concerns reaction; it does not by itself prove an identical retreat bar or dispense with §13 and other movement requirements.

## Synthetic comparison, not reachability

Assume known subject identity, sufficient Motorization Points, a real transported Supply Unit, unpinned status, applicable cohesion and an otherwise legal destination. Compare a threatened carrier with enemy mover CPA 34 versus 35; distinguish the attempted §8.5 reaction from a later §13 retreat after barrage. Then vary whether the unit begins the retreat step adjacent to an enemy combat unit, whether a friendly combat unit is stacked at each pertinent instant, and whether a destination lies in enemy ZOC. For each, compare CP exactly 15 and greater than 15 under explicitly **voluntary** and independently established **forced** facts. A result requires a chosen interpretation of the abstract carrier, cargo coupling, movement/escort and ZOC rules; neither table threshold alone proves legal movement. Check dummy versus real cargo and privacy only under separately accepted policies. These are unexecuted test ideas, not a gameplay ruling.
