# Review: occupied-hex exception and abstract supply capture

**Disposition:** The source comparison is supported. It establishes a special exception to the general entry prohibition, but neither a general exception for Supply Units nor an automatic Land-only raider capture. No material correction found.

- Land §8.13, printed/PDF p. 14, prohibits entering a hex containing an enemy unit and expressly cross-references §27.4. Section 27.41, p. 39, describes both a desert raider entering enemy-controlled ground and an enemy combat unit entering a friendly desert raider's hex as potential spotting triggers. Section 27.44 further addresses the moving enemy unit entering the raider's hex and the cost of a spotting attempt. This specific procedure supports the note's qualification; it does not make every enemy-occupied hex enterable.
- Section 27.42 prohibits a raider entering an enemy-occupied hex except to raid. Section 27.55 permits raids on Supply Dumps, including guarded dumps, **except dumps in major cities**; it directs the raider to enter, reveal itself and expend five CP, with different procedures for guarded and unguarded targets. Section 27.56 provides for a dummy after any guards-raider conflict if a raider survives. These are special raid procedures, not a direct grant of capture-on-entry.
- Sections 32.11–.13, p. 43, support the stated Land-only supply distinction: only the supply rules below apply; a Supply Unit represents abstract supplies using a Supply Dump counter; an enemy combat unit entering its hex captures it and may use supplies immediately and freely. Representation alone does not decide which detailed raid rules survive, whether their trigger takes precedence, or the combat-unit status of a raider.
- Additional classification evidence reinforces caution: §27.14, p. 38, says LRDG has no combat ratings and cannot engage in normal combat; §27.2 says DSA operates and is formed similarly, subject to listed exceptions. The §3.1 glossary, p. 5, describes a desert raider as capable of limited attacks and defines combat unit by ability to engage units and/or aircraft in combat. Those passages constrain the question but do not expressly answer whether a §27 raid makes a raider a combat unit for §32.13; no categorical result follows without interpreting their terms and scope.
