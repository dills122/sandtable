# Independent source review: rail, ZOC and railhead

**Disposition:** Candidate accurately identifies a real interaction between §8.73's rail-specific ZOC prohibition and §10.26's all-movement-purposes treatment. Neither clause explicitly states its precedence against the other or a self-occupancy condition. Maintain both interpretations as decisions, not source-certain outcomes. Separate rail movement from rail construction: §10.26 is expressly about **movement purposes**, while §24.65 separately prohibits building or rebuilding an enemy-controlled or enemy-occupied rail hex.

## Source checks

- Land PDF/printed p. 15, §8.73: a traveler must start the Operations Stage on a rail hex, must not have expended CP for another reason during that Operations Stage, and may not be in enemy ZOC; while using rail it may not enter an enemy ZOC **or** enemy-occupied hex. The phrase covering the Operations Stage warrants a decision about later CP expenditure as well as earlier history; the candidate's shorthand “must not have spent” should not itself settle that timing. Enemy occupation remains a distinct condition whichever ZOC interpretation is chosen.
- Land p. 18, §10.26: for **all movement purposes**, the presence of a friendly combat unit in a hex negates the **effect** of enemy ZOC. It does not say that the ZOC disappears, require a different friendly unit, or specify rail. Section 10.27 recognizes reciprocal ZOCs and a hex controlled by both players; §10.23's retreat provision explicitly points to §10.26. Section 10.29 says a truck convoy may not enter an enemy ZOC unless the hex is **already occupied** by a friendly combat unit; it also separately restricts non-combat units entering unoccupied enemy ZOC. That timing language directly governs the listed cases, not necessarily all rail passengers. Candidate appropriately flags the circular-entry and self-origin questions.
- Land p. 16, §§8.76–.78: destroyed rail hexes cannot be entered by rail until repaired and unbuilt rail cannot be used; pick-up and drop-off anywhere on a rail line remain subject to stacking, directional and CP restrictions; Commonwealth rail use of hexes west of an Axis-combat-unit-occupied rail hex is separately barred. The last is not restricted to the occupied hex's own ZOC. Do not treat removal of a ZOC effect as automatic permission for rail use or proof of a connected track.
- Land p. 37, §§24.61–.67: only 10th and 13th New Zealand railroad construction companies build new railroad; eligible engineers and Commonwealth HQ with engineering capability may repair destroyed rail. Section 24.65 bars building or rebuilding enemy-controlled **or** enemy-occupied rail hexes. Section 24.67 says extension must proceed westward from the last completed hex toward Tobruk without skipping; unbuilt railroad hexes serve no function until built. Even if §10.26 permits a rail move through a friendly-occupied ZOC under an accepted interpretation, it does not automatically permit building there: control and movement effects require separate rule analysis.
- Scenario PDF p. 4 / printed p. 28, §60.7 explicitly places the initial rail end at Mersa Matruh D3714. It does not alone certify track continuity, every rail hex, control, damage, or a legal six-turn extension. Scenario scan SHA-256 `fa12488e59552d7b0ff0d5abefa891d4b81af0492256bec2d25f8d61ef040a66`.
- September 1979 errata PDF p. 1 §8.73 adds a stage-wide prohibition on **earning reorganization points** for units traveling by rail; §8.71 refers to Axis use under §54.4. Neither printed correction selects a ZOC precedence. A text search of this September errata found no §10.26 or §24.6 entry; it does not exclude other authority or an indirectly relevant rule.

For observation rules, Land p. 18 §10.16 requires non-phasing disclosure of whether a potentially relevant unit exerts a ZOC when a phasing unit starts a **Movement Segment** in or moves into the hex. Do not assume this directly governs the separately named rail phase without checking sequence and applicability; nor does it license revealing hidden data in every rejected command. Candidate's need for a side-safe disclosure contract stands.
