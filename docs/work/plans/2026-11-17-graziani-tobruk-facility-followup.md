# Tobruk facility: source and errata follow-up

**Disposition:** No newly identified authoritative correction settles the post-control facility type. Retain the [bounded lifecycle proposal](2026-11-17-graziani-tobruk-facility-lifecycle-policy.md) as unadmitted; its initial single temporary facility is a project choice, not proof of later conversion or continued classification.

An [independent source review](2026-11-17-graziani-tobruk-facility-followup-review.md)
also notes §24.82's explicit engineer rebuilding permission and the adjacent
errata §25.15 cross-reference correction. Neither settles the control-change
transition or specifies a complete recovery procedure after enemy presence.

## Source distinctions

[Scenario §60.33](https://spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_Scenarios_DupFromAirRules.pdf), printed p. 27 / PDF p. 3, names a temporary Tobruk facility. [Land §22.31](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), p. 35, names a major Tobruk facility for whoever controls it. The latter gives a controller condition, but neither clause says that a temporary facility converts on a change of control, that a separate major facility emerges, or that the scenario exception lasts only while the initial side controls the hex. These remain competing interpretations; shared control under §10.27 adds a further case. The [prior independent review](2026-11-17-graziani-tobruk-facility-lifecycle-policy-review.md) records the separate source and event questions.

Land §24.81, p. 37, distinguishes temporary facilities potentially destroyed by enemy-unit presence from major facilities not destroyed *in that manner*. Presence is not equivalent to control change or an enemy ZOC, and “may” does not specify mandatory destruction, actor, timing, ownership reference, or subsequent status. Construction is a separate action: §24.83 gives new temporary construction requirements; §§24.85 and 24.87 govern permitted locations, limits, and enemy control. Rebuilding is distinct again: §24.84 addresses major-city levels reduced by bombardment, whereas §24.87 permits facilities to be rebuilt or dismantled in enemy-controlled hexes. Those permissions do not supply a universal recovery procedure for a temporary facility destroyed by enemy presence. Dismantling under §§24.86 and 24.88 is likewise not automatic destruction or a control-change conversion. Vehicle repair eligibility and repair-table effects remain separate from all these facility transitions.

## Errata and outside interpretations

The [September 1979 errata](https://www.spigames.net/db_pages/ERR_CampaignforNorthAfrica.pdf), PDF p. 2, corrects §22.34's cross-reference and §22.8's die-roll additions. Those corrections matter to later repair resolution but do not decide facility identity or classification. Review of that four-page errata identifies no correction specifically addressing §§22.31, 24.81–.88 or 60.33; this is limited to that document, not a claim that no other correction exists.

Two unaffiliated restatements illustrate different scope choices, not authoritative errata or evidence of designer intent. [CNA Living Rules](https://basmith.net/cna/rules/80-engineering) labels its scenario-list precedence as ruling R-099, describing major facilities by the general list unless scenario setup lists them otherwise. That statement alone does not specify a Tobruk transition on control change. [NJHarman's house rules](https://friendorfoe.com/war/cfna/houserules/) explicitly call Tobruk temporary and list Tripoli, Alexandria and Cairo as the only major facilities; the page identifies this as an interpretation. Neither source establishes destruction timing, rebuilding after enemy presence, or the project's adopted policy.

Before admission, choose explicit ruleset-versioned answers for post-control type and identity, shared control, enemy-presence destruction and recovery, damage and ownership persistence, and legal repair prerequisites. Keep reachability and privacy review separate; no result here proves a six-turn control change.
