# Independent review: proposed Commonwealth rail ZOC policy

**Disposition:** Defer admission. Candidate correctly labels its broad-versus-specific rule hierarchy and pre-entry snapshot as project choices, keeps occupancy and track restrictions separate, and requires side-safe behavior. The proposed self-occupancy reading has an additional source tension that needs explicit disposition: §10.26 is written for **all movement purposes**, while Land §§10.23–.24 and 8.62–.65 impose ZOC/contact movement effects on combat units already present. Reading self-presence as sufficient generally could negate those effects. A rail-only selection avoids silently changing other actions but does not itself explain why the same source sentence is treated differently elsewhere.

## Findings

1. **Source hierarchy and origin:** Land p. 15 §8.73 says a rail traveler may not begin in, or enter, enemy ZOC; p. 18 §10.26 negates enemy ZOC's **effect** for all movement purposes where a friendly combat unit is present. Both candidate's broad reading and a rail-specific override remain interpretations. The self-occupancy consequence is material, not merely textual: §10.23 says a unit starting a Movement Segment in enemy-controlled hex may leave at a break-off cost; §10.24 restricts direct movement between controlled hexes; §§8.62 and 8.65 describe contact and a two-CP break-off. Analyze how the self-origin rule relates to those clauses and to rail's no-other-CP condition before accepting it. Do not assume §10.26's broad words necessarily select self over a distinct protector, or assume its rail-only construction is already reconciled with general movement. If no coherent source interpretation is selected, retain the alternative and fail-closed gate.
2. **Entry and snapshot:** §10.29 requires **already occupied** by a friendly combat unit for truck convoy entry; it does not expressly extend that timing to rail. Excluding all in-action travelers from the pre-action occupant snapshot is a conservative proposal rather than printed rail text. It may forbid a dropoff sequence that another interpretation would allow. Land §8.77 expressly allows rail pickup and dropoff along the line subject to stacking, direction and CP; §8.72 allows one stack each direction per Operations Stage, with §8.74 repeating the once-per-direction rule. Define the physical rail-phase command and sequencing contract, including whether a completed prior action is even available within that allowance, before using sequential examples as positive cases. The candidate already conditions a prior action on other legality; preserve that condition.
3. **Tri-state logic:** For this gate, certified no ZOC passes regardless of occupancy; known qualifying friendly presence passes even with unknown ZOC; known ZOC plus known lack of a qualifying occupant fails; unknown ZOC or occupant status otherwise yields unknown. Clarify that “known lack” requires complete occupant identity/classification coverage for that hex and action snapshot, not an empty partial list. Classification, incoming ZOC coverage and terrain barriers must be independently certified. A passing gate is never proof of a legal or safe route.
4. **Physical phase and privacy:** Candidate correctly does not automatically extend §10.16's Movement Segment disclosure to the separate rail phase. An internal unknown/fail distinction must not leak through action availability, response, event contents, timing or subsequent visible effects to observers with identical authorized histories. Holding the relevant **public boundary** unavailable independent of hidden state is a sound fail-closed option while behavior is unspecified; ensure any cached list, action rejection and later visible state also respect that invariant. It is not sufficient merely to return a generic error after a successful hidden-state-dependent action creates a visible difference. Distinguish the authoritative player from other audiences, and define what is legally observable before selecting an actual command contract.
5. **Independent railhead and readiness gates:** §8.76 bars travel into destroyed rail and use of unbuilt rail; §8.78 imposes its own Axis-occupied-rail westward restriction. Scenario §60.7 supplies Matruh D3714 as initial end, not full track connectivity, control or clear seam evidence. §24.65's enemy-controlled or occupied construction/rebuilding prohibition is not erased by movement-only §10.26. Source coverage, map topology, control, stack/capacity, stage-wide CP and reorganization-point restrictions, legal-action boundaries, deterministic replay/versioning and privacy remain separate blockers. No readiness change follows from accepting this local proposal.

## Candidate test refinements

After an independently accepted policy, compare lone combat origin, separate combat protector, non-combat origin and intermediate/destination cases against both the rail gate and ordinary movement/contact expectations. Test origin protection by another selected traveler only where co-location, representation and the one-stack rail contract are established. Cover incomplete friendly occupancy separately from certified absence, and the no-ZOC/unknown-occupancy short circuit. Include phased pickup/dropoff, direction use and stage rollover without presuming multiple rail actions are legal. Hidden-state paired tests must check both participants' observable trajectories and command/event outputs. An enemy-occupied entry with a friendly occupant is a synthetic guard, not evidence that such coexistence is a reachable state.
