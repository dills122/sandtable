# Independent source review: abstract ammo and anti-armor

**Disposition:** Candidate correctly distinguishes printed §32.21 values from unresolved aggregation and charging. Its broad-assault reading has stronger direct source support than it cites: Land §3.1 explicitly defines an assault as comprising anti-armor followed by close assault. This does not by itself resolve separate debits, partial battalions or actual firing and participation; no new rate or amount is selected here.

| Source | Independent check |
| --- | --- |
| §32.21–.22 | Printed non-phasing battalion-equivalent costs are one Ammo Point to participate in an **Assault** and two in **Barrage**; a phasing unit requires twice the appropriate non-phasing amount. Supply is counted by individual battalion-equivalent components, rather than one parent counter, and ammo is expended at the instant of combat. Text has no separately labeled anti-armor amount and no detailed split/target/repetition accounting. |
| **§3.1 glossary, printed p. 5** | Defines Assault as ground combat whose two parts are **Anti-Armor Combat, then Close Assault Combat**. This directly strengthens the candidate's broad interpretation; characterize it as source evidence for applying the assault category, rather than saying the sole support is a terminological inference from §11.21. It still does not state if a unit in both steps is charged once, twice, by participating fraction, or according to another accounting unit. |
| §11.21, p. 19 | In its **capability-point** context, source says any form of Assault “(Anti-Tank, Close)” except Probe. The candidate quotes this terminology accurately. The related §11.22 uses “Anti-Armor or Close Assault”; §14.0 is headed **Anti-Armor Combat**. Do not recast quoted “Anti-Tank” as the literal wording, but the glossary and adjacent rules substantially support linking it to anti-armor in this context. CP amounts and exceptions do not themselves fix ammo. |
| §14.24 and §14.0/.26, p. 21 | §14.24 says ammunition for Anti-Armor fire is removed as expended, without an abstract quantity. §14.0/.26 allow splitting TOE points between anti-armor and assault while preventing a given point being used for both in the same Combat Segment. The general expenditure language does not itself establish a Land-only debit because §32.11 replaces other usage/expenditure rules; it does not specify a separate §32.21 debit or mechanically price both allocations. §14.23 permits a conditional reassignment of up to half, rounded down, if the defending player has no armor; do not silently assume assigned points fired. |
| §§12.15–.18, p. 20 | Forward artillery may split its TOE fire among targets and may participate in Anti-Tank or Close Assault in the same segment with required ratings and ammo. Back artillery may not coordinate with another unit or split fire. These rules show possible activities, not whether abstract ammo is assessed per firing unit, target, role or segment. |
| §8.25 and §15.0/.16/.23 | Repeated attacks in one Operations Stage are possible if necessary ammunition is held. §15.0 describes a probe as modified close assault and says close assault expends ammo for both sides. §15.16/.23 prohibit assaulting a given hex more than once per Combat Segment. These do not independently provide the missing abstract debit count. |
| September errata §§50.12 and 50.2 | §50.12 calls the relevant assault “either anti-armor or close” in a no-ammo surrender correction. §50.2 states one ammo per infantry TOE point used in its detailed-logistics context. Neither explicitly amends §32.21 or authorizes importing that detailed rate into Land-only costs. |

For a known single full battalion participating solely in the specified attack, §32.21 gives the candidate's non-phasing/phasing values of 1/2 for Assault and 2/4 for Barrage. Those examples do not resolve whether being subject to anti-armor fire, allocating but not firing, or splitting a battalion counts as participation for a particular debit. A zero-cost anti-armor action is not justified merely because the abstract rule lacks a separate label; conversely the glossary does not mandate an extra full debit on each of two component steps. Keep these outcomes unresolved until a source-qualified policy is chosen.
