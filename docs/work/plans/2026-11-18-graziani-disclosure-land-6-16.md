# FG-01: Land §§6–16 disclosure pass

**Scope:** Bounded visual pass over [Land rules](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf) printed/PDF pp. 12–27, covering §§6–16, for explicit secret choices, announcements, required disclosures and information-denial triggers. Compare the existing [§3.61–.62 inventory](2026-11-18-graziani-limited-intelligence-source-inventory.md), which already covers §10.16 and §§16.32/.51–.55. The cases below extend that inventory; a printed obligation for one player at one trigger is not a general event schema, public-state rule, disclosure to a remote observer, or proof of an actual Graziani combat. Interactions with the partial §3.62 addendum remain explicit.

| Source / event | Viewer, trigger and printed information | Limits and relation to existing inventory |
| --- | --- | --- |
| §11.0 procedure, p. 18 | Before combat resolution, **each player** secretly records Forward/Back positions of eligible gun units; each player also secretly notes barrage targets. Later both players **secretly** assign units to Anti-Armor and/or Close Assault, potentially withholding eligible units. | The text specifies secret choices and their sequence, not exactly when all choices are revealed, a simultaneous-commit protocol, or visibility of withheld choices. §5.2 orders position, barrage, retreat, force assignment, anti-armor and close assault; secret positioning is not a permanent ban on every later consequence. |
| §11.36, p. 19 | The players keep a running record of combat strength for parent formations; it says strengths are secret from opposing players and revealed **only upon attack**. | Its “strengths” and “attack” wording needs reconciliation with §3.62's limited type disclosures and individual barrage selection. It does not identify an exact electronic audience, retrospective access, full formation contents or the meaning of every combat trigger. |
| §§12.23–.24, p. 20 | Barraging player states which **hexes he wishes to Barrage from**; the opposing player states how many targets, individual battalions/companies, are in **each hex adjacent to a firing hex** and whether each is Gun, Armor or Infantry class. That player need not state strength, exact type or unit designation. A specifically known unit from patrol or previous combat may be designated; otherwise fire is “blind” by type and assigned temporary ID numbers. | The procedure distinguishes an adjacent-target count/class response from the earlier hidden target choice and from a generic world query. The specific-known-unit exception is not permission to infer unreported information or identify all contents. §12.22's target exceptions apply; do not turn adjacent-hex reporting into an unrestricted stack inventory. |
| §12.0 procedure, p. 20 | On barrage resolution the barraging player states the target by type and temporary ID, or by specific identity if already known, and the number of Actual Barrage Points directed at it. | This later declaration, identified in the [independent review](2026-11-18-graziani-disclosure-land-6-16-review.md), is distinct from §11.0's earlier secret planning. It does not itself reveal every weapon or full formation strength. |
| §14.23, p. 21 | The player directing Anti-Armor fire **may not know** whether the opposing hex has armor. If the defending player has no units with Armor Protection in the hex being fired on, the firing player may reassign up to one-half, rounded down, of those Anti-Armor points to Close Assault for that Combat Segment. | This is a conditional procedure under uncertainty, not an explicit general instruction to disclose all armor, every weapon or a positive/negative query at any time. §3.62's anti-armor type/presence reporting applies in its own stated circumstances. |
| §15.94, p. 25; §5.2 III.G.3, p. 12 | The probing player **does not have to announce** that an assault is a Probe until after the assault is over; units are assigned to Close Assault before Anti-Armor. The sequence separately says player A determines which assaults are probes and announces which after each is resolved. | A delayed announcement is not evidence that all actual allocations or earlier observations are hidden forever; reconcile source steps and §3.62 disclosure without publishing a probe merely on secret selection. This is timing permission, not proof that a particular probe occurred. |
| §16.0 procedure and §16.33, p. 26 | The phasing patroller announces the number of Patrol Points allocated and the hex being patrolled. If **no Patrol Points survive**, the player goes no further and receives **no information** from that patrol. If points survive, the reconnaissance procedure and §16.51 report can follow. | Existing inventory covers the report and the separate §16.32 no-combat-units notice. The [review](2026-11-18-graziani-disclosure-land-6-16-review.md) notes §16.34 separately calls for an Objective Loss Table roll after all patrol units are eliminated; reconcile that follow-on with §16.33's wording rather than assuming all processing ends. Do not undo the earlier notice or award a §16.51 report after complete loss. |
| §§16.44–.46, pp. 26–27 | A player may give a Dummy Tank Battalion an identification actually used by another tank battalion in play. During reconnaissance against its hex, the owner may identify it as an `X` tank battalion of any desired TOE strength; §16.45 says the patroller cannot know it is a dummy until attack. §16.46 provides separate elimination triggers. | This is a deception exception to an assumed always-truthful unit report, not proof every dummy is legal at a particular hex or that its identity can be globally concealed after every other source trigger. §16.43 restricts Italian formations, and §16.42's Stores cost and §32.11 mode interaction remain open. See [dummy patrol follow-up](2026-11-18-graziani-patrol-dummy-followup.md). |

## Coverage and evidence

The pass visually skimmed all printed pages 12–27 for explicit information words and the combat/patrol procedures; the table records nonduplicative material found, **not** an exhaustive semantic proof that every indirect inference or later case is accounted for. Existing §3.62 anti-armor and close-assault entries remain in force as written in that inventory, including type-only versus withheld numbers and losses. Its earlier §10.16 ZOC and §16.32/.51–.55 report entries are not reclassified as new findings. Land §32.0 supplies the Land-only Air/Logistics abstraction; any separate use of detailed supplies in §16 requires its own mode decision, and information rules do not establish a legal patrol, combatant or ammunition stock.

Searched extracted [September errata](https://www.spigames.net/db_pages/ERR_CampaignforNorthAfrica.pdf) for numbered §§11–16 entries: §12.44 clarifies the battalion-level target of a pin; §16.11 adds patrol-capable types. Other numbered entries change combat calculations; this bounded search found no directly numbered §11.36, §12.23/.24, §14.23, §15.94 or §16.33/.44/.45 correction. Indirect or later corrections are not excluded.
