# City fortifications: scenario setup and later lifecycle

**Disposition:** §60.7's unqualified initial absence conflicts with reading every built-in city fortification as active at setup. The heading and Land's distinction between built-in levels and additional construction support retaining inherent city levels as a bounded planning interpretation, but neither source explicitly says the scenario exception excludes city levels. A zero-at-setup alternative must also define the subsequent lifecycle: the inspected text does not say a zeroed city automatically regenerates at the next stage.

## Competing setup choices and lifecycle implications

1. **Built-in city levels retained at setup:** treat §60.7 as excluding separately constructed initial fortifications, reading its construction heading together with §§24.41 and 25.0. This is the existing [bounded planning choice](2026-11-17-graziani-facilities-independent-decision.md). The initial exceptions remain a source-hierarchy interpretation, not explicit scenario language. A later valid attack can reduce a city to zero; no permanent or automatically restoring city bonus follows. Rebuilding needs source-defined conditions and accepted game-mode costs.
2. **All fortification effects absent initially:** give §60.7's unqualified wording priority, including city levels. This is also plausible from the printed words. Distinguish *not initially present* from a level actually **destroyed** by bombardment or barrage: §§24.44–.45 prohibit construction from scratch in a major city but permit rebuilding destroyed levels. The source passages inspected do not explicitly resolve whether the scenario-created zero qualifies for rebuilding. It is not enough to say “zero now, restore normal city level next stage,” nor to assume the destroyed-level procedure applies. A project choosing this alternative must version the setup and later restoration/build eligibility explicitly.

Both readings require independently certified per-hex city identity, not mere naming in scenario tables. No evidence here proves a particular city is attacked, reduced, rebuilt or reachable in six turns. Combat, construction and repair-facility behavior need their own contracts; an inherent city level is neither an indestructible terrain attribute nor automatically an extra counter stacked with another fortification.
