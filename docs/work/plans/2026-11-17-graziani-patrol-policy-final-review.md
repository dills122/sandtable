# Patrol history policy: final planning review

**Disposition:** Revised `gra-patrol-history-v1-proposed` is coherent as a bounded, explicitly selected planning interpretation. Its known-failure/unknown logic and separate causal-closure condition no longer contradict each other. This is **not runtime admission**: source alternatives remain live, and no completed event, closure, privacy, replay or full patrol contract has been demonstrated.

## Source and selected behavior

Visually rechecked [Land rules](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf) PDF/printed pp. 12, 26 and 43, §§5.2, 16.0, 16.16–.17, 16.21–.22 and 32.25. Land SHA-256 `b362870368b9fb8abe6918195fdcf878d4a8041c557b835b9a1bdde3c8b76e99`; September errata `5db539e1fac4aea3ef152370fb732f1938307fe4b2d47ad93eb605db0a09f0fb`. Same source packages as earlier reviews, not independent editions. Errata was not exhaustively re-read in this pass.

- §16.0's general rule refers to an Operations Stage in which no anti-armor or close-assault combat has taken place. §16.21 instead describes a phasing player who does not engage against enemy units. Section 5.2 phase L independently phrases the permission around the phasing player not having engaged in assault, and schedules B's F–L after A's L. Both players can participate in A's anti-armor step. These texts give real support to an actor/participant-sensitive alternative as well as to the selected stage-wide past-event interpretation; the latter is a planning choice and does not prove what “engage” means.
- The selected causal cut permits A's patrol followed by B's later combat; it blocks B after completed A combat. This is internally consistent as an online **past-event** policy, but a literal whole-stage prohibition could instead constrain later combat or require some other mechanism. Do not present the proposal as a proof that the original rules authorize A's patrol regardless of B's later actions.
- §16.21 allows patrol despite artillery barrage but prohibits reconnoitering a hex that has undergone any barrage or air bombardment. Its target clause does not explicitly repeat a time limit or actor. Limiting target history to the current stage and treating either side's attack as relevant are reasonable disclosed choices, not literal reset or actor text. Retain full history and target identity so another version remains reproducible.
- §16.16's normal patrol supplies and §32.25's explicit Land-only no-expenditure rule are distinguished correctly. This exception does not establish point eligibility, target/path validity, weather, CP, casualty handling or later disclosure.

## Causal closure and privacy

The existential predicates are logically consistent: a verified disqualifier is an internal known failure even with unrelated incomplete evidence; without a verified witness, incomplete relevant coverage prevents a pass. Candidate separately requires closure for the current cut before committing an action **or publishing an eligibility decision**, including an internally known failure. That is an intentional stronger operational gate, not a contradiction. A closure proof must include all causally prior producers and incomplete air/combat work applicable to the cut; it must not wait for B's future phase before A can act. Classification of zero-effect, failed, cancelled and partially executed actions remains a contract prerequisite, not a source fact supplied by the proposal.

The privacy warning is substantive: generic responses alone cannot hide execution versus refusal when resulting disclosures, casualties, RNG use or later states differ. An authorized observation model and complete capability/command protocol must establish indistinguishability for each audience with equivalent authorized history; otherwise gate the capability at a boundary independent of hidden facts. This review approves no particular disclosure or timing mechanism. Store the event cut, producer completion evidence, predicates, selected version and any correction semantics for deterministic replay; do not infer absent history from missing logs.
