# Printed distance example: Tobruk and Ben Gania

**Date:** November 18, 2026. Follow-up to [map registration source check](2026-11-18-graziani-map-registration-source-next.md) and the [distance decision](2026-11-17-graziani-map-distance-decision.md). Air/Logistics p. 24's other worked example gives a **31-hex** result for Tobruk to Ben Gania, with two named 31-hex axis constructions. It is internally compatible with the 33-column candidate and the neighboring printed distance chart. It is not an independent edition, an edge-registration observation, or a proof of a land route.

## Source and bounded comparison

- “Determining Shortest Distance,” Example 1, states **31 hexes west to B4809** or **31 southwest to B1725** from Tobruk, with Ben Gania on the northwest–southeast axis intersecting both constructions. Section 37.42 on the next PDF page identifies Tobruk as `C4807`, Ben Gania as `B3715`, and their printed distance as **31**. The example and chart use the same source package; agreement is not independent publisher corroboration.
- Under the explicitly conditional **33-column eastward translation** and odd-row-west stagger described in the [seam geometry comparison](2026-11-17-graziani-seam-geometry-comparison.md), all three distances from `C4807` to `B4809`, `B1725`, and `B3715` calculate as **31**. The two B-sheet construction points lie 11 and 20 regular-grid steps respectively from `B3715` along the same diagonal, summing to 31. This is an arithmetic model check of the printed construction, not proof that a player travels through either construction point to reach Ben Gania: doing that would add a further leg.
- The same-row westward leg is a useful discriminator **within this particular family of models**. If adjacent-sheet eastward translation is `N` columns, the row-aligned separation from `C4807` to `B4809` is `N − 2`: `N = 32, 33, 34` gives **30, 31, 32** respectively. Thus the example's 31 favors `N = 33` among those candidates, assuming the named cells and ordinary regular-grid geometry. It does not select among all possible registration models, identify overlapping printed partial cells, or decide which seam sides actually exist. The second southwest leg alone does not discriminate these three offsets: the same model gives 31 for N=32 and N=33, and 32 for N=34.
- Air p. 24 expressly calls this a shortest distance, **not the actual flight path**. Air §37.42's chart and its assumed sea grid are distance aids; they do not prescribe ground traversal, feature continuity or a playable cross-sheet edge. The [distance decision](2026-11-17-graziani-map-distance-decision.md) documents other chart contradictions, so this agreeing example cannot validate every printed cell or choose a correction. Land §4.1's `01xx`/`39xx` overlay wording remains unresolved.

For reproducibility of the **model arithmetic only**, let `sheet` be 0 for A, 1 for B, and so on; use `x = 2 × (column + 33 × sheet) − (row mod 2)`, `y = row`, and hex distance `max(|Δy|, (|Δx| + |Δy|)/2)`. It yields the stated 31 / 11 / 20 values for the four labeled cells. Those numbers depend on the candidate translation and stagger, not on independent physical-sheet registration. No graph edge, alias, unit movement, scenario event or authority decision follows.
