# Fleet rest and repair: existing policy audit

**Result:** [RUL-GRA-15](2026-11-12-graziani-ruling-decisions.md#rul-gra-15-abstract-fleet-attack-order-planning-interpretation) selects **repair before abstract fleet attack** for planning, including a repair completion in the same stage. It neither selects nor contradicts counting the same Alexandria stages toward both mandatory post-bombardment rest and six-stage repair. That clock-overlap question, the repair-stage accrual boundary and reset on damage remain open. The [review](2026-11-18-graziani-fleet-repair-policy-audit-review.md) notes the ruling requires admission contracts and damage/repair ordering, while its linked proposal requires repair prerequisites; it does not literally require a separately named standalone repair contract.

## Source and policy boundaries

- [Land §32.2](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), p. 43, excludes the Commonwealth Fleet from supplies **for that case's purposes**. [Section 30.37](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), p. 42, independently requires six **consecutive** Operations Stages in Alexandria for one Gun Rating level and says no engineer or supplies are needed; moving before completion restarts work. §30.36 says elimination of the entire Gun Rating sinks the ship, which is then unrepairable. [Fleet repair source review](2026-11-18-graziani-fleet-repair-supply-source-review.md) verifies these points; neither supply exemption cancels the temporal conditions.
- Section 30.16, p. 41, describes two port stages per stage in action and a maximum of three consecutive at sea. Section 30.25, p. 42, separately requires a ship after offshore bombardment to spend its next **two** stages in Alexandria, except that AA fire is excluded from that rule. These identify separate duties; the inspected clauses do not expressly say whether the same two stages count toward §30.37 repair, or require them to be disjoint. Section 30.38 permits AA fire during repair; it does not settle AA restoration or rest-credit accounting.
- RUL-GRA-15 adopts the [fleet sequence proposal](2026-11-17-graziani-fleet-sequence-next.md): secret plot in Fleet Assignment, complete Fleet Repair, then abstract attack before first Reserve Designation. The proposal explicitly says a repair completion in the same stage applies **before** the attack. This is a planning selection for attack/repair order, not source-proven placement of §32.73's missing Tactical Naval Movement Segment. It says to respect applicable repair-progress rules and leaves the completion predicate, clock overlap and damage reset unselected. The [sequence review](2026-11-17-graziani-fleet-sequence-review.md) confirms the sequencing gap and admission requirements; it does not make overlap a decided policy.

## Decision inputs, not a selected rule

Given an **already damaged, unsunk ship** with an otherwise eligible six-stage continuous Alexandria stay, assume its two required post-bombardment Alexandria stages are the first two of that stay. Under a concurrent-credit interpretation, stages 1 and 2 advance both duties and stage 6 can complete repair, subject to the selected accrual boundary. Under a disjoint-credit interpretation, they satisfy rest but do not advance repair; six subsequent qualifying stages are needed, completion no earlier than stage 8 under that specific convention. The latter is an illustration, not a rule that the source requires rest before repair or forbids other disjoint orderings. Both examples assume the ship remains continuously in Alexandria and no intervening event invalidates either clock; neither is an actual scenario witness.

A contract needs to specify (1) whether and when a stage credits each duty, including partial-stage arrival/departure; (2) what resets or pauses repair, separately for moving, a new hit while still in port, and sinking; (3) whether a completed rest obligation changes later availability; and (4) whether repair and rest may share a qualifying stage. Preserve RUL-GRA-15's **already selected** completion-before-attack order when its completion predicate is met; test the clock predicate separately instead of reopening that ordering silently. Contrast concurrent and disjoint counts, an interrupted stay, attack without departure, sinking, and a potential sixth-stage completion before an attack. These are unexecuted comparison vectors, not approved policy, a proved damage event or a six-turn repair.
