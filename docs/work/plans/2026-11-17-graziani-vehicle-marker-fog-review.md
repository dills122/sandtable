# Independent review: vehicle marker contents and visibility

**Disposition:** Core finding is supported: Land addendum §3.6 makes marker
contents secret while allowing inspection of map counter stacks. Capture,
repair and historical disclosures need separate audience rules. Two source
scope corrections matter: the partial-tow addendum expressly names broken-down
vehicles, and the two capture relocation clauses use different wording.

## Source checks and corrections

1. **Map and hidden contents:** Land p. 45 addendum §3.6 says map stacks may
   be examined but counter contents are another matter; its vehicle entry
   expressly says broken-down vehicle, destroyed tank and abandoned vehicle
   marker contents are secret. The accompanying anti-armor entry discloses
   participating tank/SP-gun types and armored-recce/halftrack presence, but
   not numbers, origins or which TOE points absorb damage. Prior disclosed
   observations need not be forgotten, but neither that entry nor visible marker
   presence makes its contents public. §21.42 p. 34 requires a numbered broken
   vehicle marker plus owning-player record; number visibility and linkability
   are separate from disclosure of its contents.
2. **Partial towing scope:** §21.63 p. 34 permits both broken-down vehicle
   and destroyed tank markers to be towed under its rules. Its p. 45 addendum
   expressly permits **any portion of the broken-down vehicles** in a hex to
   be towed and requires a new marker and record adjustment if only some are
   moved. The reviewed note's table says “any portion of a marker's vehicles”
   without limiting that addendum to broken-down vehicles. It should state
   the narrower source scope; a partial split of a **destroyed tank** marker
   needs separate authority or an explicitly selected interpretation. The
   addendum does not itself reveal the split marker's composition.
3. **Capture and relocation wording:** §§21.52–.55 require a battalion-size
   or larger friendly combat unit to *move adjacent* to the broken-down marker,
   subject to the marker owner's friendly protection/control and passable
   adjacency. §21.55 says captured breakdowns may be placed a **maximum of
   three hexes** away at capture, subject to the specified route restrictions.
   §14.52–.53 instead covers enemy entry into a destroyed-tank marker's hex
   with no other friendly combat unit there, and captor choice of permanent
   elimination or capture; §14.53 says captured destroyed tanks “may be moved
   three hexes.” Treating that as **up to** three is plausible but not
   textually identical to §21.55's maximum wording. Retain exact wording and
   resolve whether shorter relocation is allowed before enforcing either
   interpretation. Each case has its own path and protection conditions;
   do not substitute one capture procedure for the other.
4. **Repair and identity:** §§22.11–.14, .23–.25, .33–.34 and .5 require
   control and other conditions, distinguish repair categories, keep captured
   vehicles separate as specified in §22.14, and treat repaired vehicles as
   replacements without training. §14.51 says only destroyed tanks may be
   captured and other destroyed vehicles are removed immediately. None of
   these clauses specifies a blanket public publication of every lot field
   or the captor's complete post-capture knowledge. Conversely, a policy
   cannot assume the captor learns nothing even when a lawful repair choice
   needs type information: define minimum authorized information and timing.
5. **Observable marker events:** The proposed equal-view tests are appropriate
   only for histories with identical authorized observations and outward
   actions. Splitting a marker can visibly create another counter under
   addendum §21.63, so equal hidden lots do not imply equal event streams if
   the public marker movements differ. For equivalent outward histories, test
   that stable identifiers, error behavior, action availability, event order
   and projections do not reveal hidden composition beyond explicitly accepted
   disclosures. Different authorized repair choices and action availability may
   require a separately designed disclosure or mediated decision; the source
   alone does not resolve this tension.

The reviewed note correctly limits §32.55's no-breakdown rule to
Motorization Points and does not settle abandoned-vehicle capture. No specific
abandoned capture procedure, original starting marker or real game event is
inferred. Source locations and values were checked in the same edition.

## Conditional tests

- For two known identical visible broken-down markers with different private
  composition, compare unauthorized views and metadata under the same
  authorized outward action. Preserve prior legally disclosed participant
  types without reporting which types absorbed losses.
- Tow part of a mixed **broken-down** lot: add a marker and reconcile private
  quantities per §21.63 addendum. Repeat with a destroyed tank lot as an
  explicitly unsupported or separately ruled case, not an automatic source
  permission.
- Contrast §21.55's maximum-three relocation with §14.53's “three hexes”
  wording, and test no movement, shorter and full three only after adopting a
  case-specific interpretation. Apply protective-unit and passable-side checks
  for breakdown separately from destroyed-tank hex-entry and occupancy checks.
- For captured type-dependent repair, verify the authorized decision can be
  made without revealing unrelated models or quantities. Retain private
  authoritative identities and exact-once state across recovery; determine
  actual revelation from an accepted source/policy rather than from this test.
