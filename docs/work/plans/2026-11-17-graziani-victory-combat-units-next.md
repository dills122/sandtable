# Graziani victory: combat-unit classification and HQs

**Finding:** For §60.81's Italian strategic and decisive objectives, “combat
unit” is not automatically equivalent to a unit with an unparenthesized Close
Assault Rating. General Land §3.23 includes infantry, tanks, recce, artillery,
antitank and antiaircraft units; §3.31 makes an HQ counter representing an
attached combat unit a combat unit. The stricter parenthesis test occurs in
§60.82, the separate Italian Campaign victory case. Its application to
Graziani requires a separate ruling, not silent inheritance.

## What qualifies, and what remains conditional

1. §3.1 defines a combat unit by capacity to engage other units and/or
   aircraft in combat; HQs, engineers and dummy tank formations qualify in
   certain situations. §3.23 explicitly lists the six types above and notes
   that certain noncombat units count for specified determinations such as ZOC,
   reconnaissance, barrage and bombardment. A case-specific treatment does not
   alone establish engineer or dummy eligibility for a victory condition.
2. §3.31 says an HQ with **any combat units attached** is itself a combat unit
   and fights with the combined strengths represented by it. Under §3.1,
   attachment means co-location **and representation by the parent's counter**;
   organizational assignment alone can persist across different hexes and is
   not attachment. The p. 45 addendum distinguishes a parent unit's attached
   counters from a parent formation's assigned counters. Do not double-count
   a represented subordinate as a second physically placed counter, or infer
   combat status solely from a roster association.
3. §3.33 says an HQ with no combat units attached is no longer a combat unit,
   occupies zero stacking points and may use its parenthesized ratings; it may
   not voluntarily attack. §3.34 separately describes certain Italian HQs with
   artillery assigned directly, their usable combat strengths and reversion to
   noncombat HQ when all their gun TOE points are eliminated. The relationship
   between intrinsic gun TOE and §3.33's no-attached-unit wording needs its
   own case-level interpretation. §3.35 likewise gives some armored HQs
   assigned tanks usable only without attached combat units. Do not assume
   every HQ with an intrinsic parenthesized value qualifies or disqualifies in
   all circumstances; preserve special cases as unknown until resolved.
4. §32.13 says a Supply Unit has no combat capabilities. A real supply unit
   alone is not positive evidence of a qualifying combat unit. Dummy units,
   trucks, engineers, replacement counters, an empty HQ and eliminated or
   detached subunits each require the applicable source and current state;
   no generic “any counter in hex” shortcut is justified. Land-only §32.81
   removes Commonwealth AA units and points, so the general §3.23 AA category
   does not put removed units back on the map.
5. §60.81 explicitly says **a combat unit** for Italian strategic victory at
   Alexandria or Cairo, and **any combat unit** for the decisive condition
   at Mersa Matruh and Siwa, each with the condition's stated supply source.
   Other §60.81 conditions use “take and hold,” “occupy,” or “retain
   possession” and their own supply clauses; do not rewrite all six victory
   levels as one expressly identical combat-unit predicate. Conversely,
   neither a qualifying type nor an HQ's attachment proves actual possession,
   supply, path, timing or a victory result. The meaning of one unit versus
   units holding both decisive locations requires its own reading.

§60.82's parenthetical definition requires units holding its listed places to
have **non-parenthesized Close Assault Ratings**. That scenario-specific text
is evidence of a narrower campaign condition, not an express amendment of
§60.81. General combat classification and this narrower rating predicate should
be represented separately if both scenarios are modeled. The effect of an HQ's
combined subordinate ratings on §60.82 is not decided here.

## Conditional witnesses and checks

- Scenario §60.31 lists **63 Cirene Division** at C4120, with **LXIII (L)**
  marked attached and tank-type. Subject to the starting organization and any
  subsequent legal changes, that is a concrete positive witness for §3.31's
  attached-combat-HQ rule. Its starting C4120 placement is not evidence of
  occupancy of any victory city or a trace to its required supply source.
- §60.41 lists **1st Royal Northumberland Fusiliers** infantry at C3721 and
  **7th Medium Artillery Regiment** at C3922. Both are among §3.23's combat
  types when present and otherwise eligible; an artillery classification alone
  does not decide whether a scenario-specific close-assault-rating test is
  met. These are Commonwealth witnesses, not claims that either can satisfy
  an Italian-only victory condition.
- Synthetic same-hex HQ A with a combat infantry unit **attached and
  represented by A**: A qualifies under §3.31's combat classification. Change
  only the unit's status to assigned but detached and represented elsewhere:
  that association alone does not trigger §3.31. If A also has no other
  combat attachment or unresolved intrinsic combat-strength exception,
  §3.33 supplies the negative baseline. Do not count A and its represented
  subordinate as two independent occupants.
- Separately compare a combat artillery unit with the §60.82 rating predicate;
  no “all artillery has a particular close-assault rating” is assumed. Compare
  scenario-specific predicate results without importing them into §60.81.
  Include conflicting/missing attachment data as unknown and check the actual
  victory phase, side, geography and required supply route independently.

No complete victory adjudication, runtime change, or gameplay test is claimed.
An [independent source review](2026-11-17-graziani-victory-combat-units-review.md)
corroborates the bounded classifications and flags errata §3.36 on HQ defense;
that defense clarification does not by itself decide victory eligibility.
