# Patrol eligibility: operation-stage time boundary

**Disposition:** the source sequence supports evaluating A's patrol at its phase before B's later movement/combat, but the exact prior-combat and target-bombardment windows remain interpretive. This source check proposes no admitted rule and does not establish historical patrol reachability.

## Source facts

- [Land rules](https://www.spigames.net/PDFv10/CNA_LandGameRules.pdf), PDF/printed p. 12 §5.2, puts player A's Movement and Combat, truck movement, Commonwealth rail if applicable, and repair before A's Patrol Phase L. It then gives player B phases F through L, reversing A/B references. Both players' portions occur in one Operations Stage; §6.14 on the same page explicitly uses that stage across both portions for CP accounting. The next two OpStages repeat the first-stage sequence.
- Land p. 26 §16.0's general rule permits patrol in an Operations Stage in which no anti-armor or close assault combat **has taken place**. Its specific §16.21 instead describes an OpStage in which the phasing player does not engage in either against enemy units. The §5.2 phase-L summary mentions the phasing player's assault, without mentioning anti-armor. These are three different formulations; a source reading must preserve both kinds in §16.0 and §16.21 rather than use the outline as an exhaustive enumeration.
- Section 16.21 also permits patrol despite artillery barrage during a given OpStage but bars reconnaissance of a hex that has undergone any barrage or air bombardment. The target clause does not explicitly repeat the OpStage window or identify whose attack matters. The immediately preceding phrase does specify an OpStage for the general barrage allowance; that context supports, but does not prove, a same-stage target window. Section 16.22 requires an enemy-occupied target and a qualifying path, independently of history.
- [September 1979 errata](https://www.spigames.net/db_pages/ERR_CampaignforNorthAfrica.pdf), PDF p. 2 §16.11 expands patrol point eligibility; the four-page scan contains no explicit §16.0 or §16.21 correction located in this check. Other source material may exist.

## Interpretation and unresolved cases

At A's patrol boundary, §16.0's past-tense formulation and §5.2's sequence support evaluating combat **already performed**. B can react during A's movement and both players participate in A's combat steps, but B has not yet chosen its own later phase-G actions. The sequence alone does not conclusively exclude a whole-stage constraint. Requiring proof that B will not later attack, retrospectively canceling A's already-resolved patrol when B attacks, or silently forbidding B's attack after A patrols would each require an additional forward constraint or procedure not specified in these passages. Select and version an explicit decision rather than treating this observation as a complete formal algorithm.

For B, an A-initiated anti-armor or close assault earlier in the same stage necessarily satisfies §16.0's broad occurrence wording. It may also count as B's own engagement under §16.21 if defending constitutes engagement; the source passages do not define that word's participant scope. Section 17.25's capitalized **Engaged** is a special morale outcome, and does not itself define the ordinary verb in §16.21. A side-initiator interpretation of §16.21 would allow B to patrol if B launched neither category during B's own movement/combat, even after defending against A; a stage-wide interpretation bars it. A participation-based interpretation is a third alternative, potentially dependent on which units actually took part. Preserve initiator, defender, combat type and event timing; a single global Boolean cannot evaluate all three.

For a target bombarded earlier, compare a **current-stage** history window with the literal unbounded past-tense reading. The latter would potentially block that hex indefinitely unless another rule supplies a reset; the examined text states none. Prior-turn or earlier-stage attack, friendly versus opposing fire, and whether a hex vacated and reoccupied changes the status are useful distinguishing cases. Do not assume outcome from plausibility alone. Track the target hex and operation-stage identity, including attack type and timestamp; retain longer history until the window is decided.

## Proposed tests after a policy decision

- A patrol after no earlier anti-armor/close assault, followed by B attacking later: exercise the chosen temporal policy and avoid retroactive mutation unless explicitly specified. Repeat with initiative reversed; A/B are roles, not fixed nationalities.
- A attacks, then B is phasing: compare B's patrol under global, initiating-side, and participation-based readings, with attacks at another hex and each combat type. Test barrage only independently from anti-armor and close assault.
- Current target bombarded this stage, another hex bombarded, same target bombarded in a prior stage, and prior turn; test friendly and enemy bombardment separately. Reject or preserve unknown according to the admitted window and event coverage. Stage rollover must not erase historical facts needed by an unbounded policy.
- Test path/target and other §16 prerequisites independently, and deterministic replay with side-safe messages where underlying target history is hidden. These are synthetic unexecuted cases, not source-certified outcomes.
